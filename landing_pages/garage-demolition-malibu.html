Garage Demolition Situations in Malibu

Malibu garage demolitions don't follow a standard template. The terrain varies from flat PCH-adjacent lots to steep canyon parcels accessible only by a single switchback driveway. The regulatory environment layers LA County building requirements over Coastal Zone rules. And increasingly, fire history shapes what's left standing and what the demo scope actually involves.

Below are the four most common garage demo situations C&S handles across the Malibu corridor — and what each one requires in terms of permits, equipment, and sequencing.

LA County Permit Process for Malibu Garage Demolition

Malibu contracts building and safety services to LA County — the City of Malibu does not issue its own building permits. Here's how the permit sequence typically runs for a detached garage teardown:

  1. Asbestos & Lead Survey (SCAQMD Rule 1403)Required for all structures before any demolition permit is issued — regardless of construction date. C&S coordinates with a licensed environmental inspector. If regulated asbestos is found, SCAQMD must be notified at least 10 business days before demolition begins, and abatement precedes demo.

  2. Utility Disconnection LettersGas (SoCalGas) and electric (SCE) service to the garage must be formally terminated. Written confirmation from each utility is required as part of the permit application. Septic tie-ins, if any, require an additional clearance from the LA County Department of Public Health.

  3. Coastal Zone DeterminationMost Malibu properties are within the Coastal Zone. Standalone garage demolition often qualifies for a Coastal Act categorical exemption, but this must be confirmed with the City's planning department before permits are filed. C&S identifies the likely path during the site visit.

  4. LA County Demolition Permit ApplicationSubmitted to LA County Building & Safety with the survey report, utility letters, and site documentation. C&S prepares and submits the full application package on your behalf.

  5. Final Inspection & Permit CloseLA County requires a post-demolition inspection to confirm site clearance and proper disconnection before the permit is closed. This is required before new structure permits can be issued for the same footprint.

Fire-damaged structures: If the structure was damaged by a wildfire, additional LA County protocols and potential state emergency permitting programs may apply. Contact C&S before assuming standard permit timelines apply.

Site Conditions That Affect How the Job Gets Done

Every Malibu garage project gets assessed on its own terms. Flat PCH-adjacent lots and hillside canyon parcels are completely different jobs even for the same structure size and scope.

Coastal Flat Lots (PCH Adjacent)

Properties along Pacific Coast Highway with flat or near-flat pads generally allow standard equipment access. Compact excavators can maneuver near the structure, and debris staging is usually straightforward. The main constraint on coastal flat lots is often neighbor proximity and HOA rules around working hours and dumpster placement.

Hillside and Canyon Properties

Garages on hillside properties — Malibu Canyon, Latigo Canyon, Trancas Canyon, Las Virgenes — often have single-lane driveway access, steep grades, and retaining structures adjacent to the garage footprint. C&S uses compact excavators sized for the available access and plans the debris removal around the specific driveway constraints. We document pre-demo site conditions on all hillside jobs — photos of neighboring structures and retaining features before any equipment arrives.

Post-Fire Properties (Woolsey and Other Fire Areas)

Properties in the Woolsey fire corridor and other affected Malibu burn areas may have unusual site conditions — compromised soils, debris that wasn't fully cleared in prior cleanup phases, and structural elements that aren't what they appear. C&S assesses these properties carefully before committing to a scope and timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who issues demolition permits in Malibu?

LA County Building & Safety issues demolition permits for all structures in Malibu. The City contracts building and safety services to the County. Coastal Zone determinations involve the City of Malibu's planning department separately from the building permit itself.

Does my garage demo need a Coastal Development Permit?

Standalone garage demolition often qualifies for a Coastal Act categorical exemption — but this is determined case by case based on the project scope, site location within the Coastal Zone, and whether any grading or new construction is associated with the demo. C&S identifies the likely path during the site visit so you know what to expect before permits are filed.

How long does the permit process take?

Standard LA County demolition permits typically take 2–4 weeks once the application is complete. If asbestos is found and SCAQMD notification is required, add a minimum of 10 business days for the notification period before demo can begin. Coastal Zone review adds additional time if a CDP is required. C&S gives you a realistic timeline estimate based on your specific site during the site visit.

What happens to the concrete slab?

The slab is removed completely unless your architect's plans specify retention. Malibu properties being prepared for ADU construction sometimes retain the existing slab if the structural engineer approves its use. C&S follows your project drawings — if the plans say slab out, slab comes out; if retention is specified, we demo to grade and leave the slab intact.
