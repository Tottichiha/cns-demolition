Northwood Is Not One Neighborhood — and Your Demo Project Cannot Treat It Like One

Most demolition contractors drive to Irvine and assume every HOA is the same. In Northwood, that assumption will cost you weeks. The original Northwood section — bounded roughly by Irvine Blvd, Yale Ave, Bryan Ave, and Culver Dr — is governed by the Northwood Community Association. Northwood Pointe, the newer development further east that came largely in the 2000s, operates under its own HOA with separate board schedules, separate ARC forms, and separate submittal requirements.

Before the City of Irvine Building & Safety issues a demolition permit, you need HOA approval documentation in hand. If you submit to the wrong HOA, or miss the ARC meeting cycle, your timeline slips by a month or more. C&S Demolition coordinates this sequence as part of our standard Northwood project workflow — we know which section your property sits in and which HOA packet to prepare first.

SCAQMD Notice: Per South Coast Air Quality Management District Rule 1403, every structure in Northwood — regardless of age — requires a certified asbestos survey before a demolition permit can be issued. There is no age exemption. The original 1980s Northwood homes carry a documented risk in popcorn ceilings, floor tile mastic, and duct wrap insulation. We coordinate survey scheduling so it does not delay your permit timeline.

Why 1980s Northwood Construction Matters for Demolition Scope

The majority of original Northwood homes were built between 1978 and 1992 — right in the window when asbestos-containing materials were still widely used in residential construction. Popcorn (acoustic) ceiling texture applied before 1990 commonly contained chrysotile asbestos. The black mastic adhesive used to bond 9"×9" and 12"×12" vinyl floor tiles was a standard carrier for asbestos fibers through roughly 1985. Fiberglass duct insulation wrap and HVAC joint tape from this era can also be suspect.

None of this means a Northwood demolition is unusually difficult — it means it requires proper sequencing. A certified industrial hygienist surveys and samples, the SCAQMD receives notification, and abatement (if required) is completed before any mechanical demolition begins. C&S Demolition has worked through this sequence on dozens of 1980s-era Irvine structures. We do not surprise you with a cost increase on demo day because an asbestos floor tile was "discovered."

Northwood Pointe: Newer Homes, Different Permit Path

Northwood Pointe homes — built primarily 2001 through 2010 — have a substantially lower asbestos risk, but the permitting process involves its own nuances. Because Northwood Pointe HOA board meetings are scheduled independently of the older Northwood Community Association, ARC timelines can differ. The City of Irvine still requires demolition permits to flow through Building & Safety regardless of which HOA section the property is in, but the supporting HOA approval letter needs to come from the correct body.

Original Northwood (1978–1992 Construction)

Higher probability of ACBM in ceilings, floor tile mastic, and duct systems. ARC approval through Northwood Community Association. Permits from City of Irvine Building & Safety. IRWD and SCE utility disconnects coordinated before demo. Flat terrain on Bryan/Yale/Culver streets allows full excavator access.

Northwood Pointe (2001–2010 Construction)

Lower ACBM risk, but SCAQMD survey still required. ARC approval through Northwood Pointe HOA — separate submittal, separate board calendar. Often larger footprints with attached 2-car garages. Utility disconnects coordinated with IRWD, SCE, and SoCalGas. Newer homes may have engineered lumber and steel beam elements affecting structural sequencing.

Flat Lots and Logical Streets — What Northwood Gets Right for Demolition

Northwood sits in the relatively flat northeastern portion of Irvine, unlike hillside villages such as Turtle Rock or Quail Hill where equipment staging requires extra planning. Bryan Ave, Yale Ave, Culver Dr, and Irvine Blvd form a grid that allows large excavators and debris trucks to route without tight turning radii or slope constraints. This translates directly to lower mobilization costs and faster demo sequences compared to hillside Irvine properties.

The proximity to The Marketplace shopping corridor along Irvine Blvd also means debris trucks have clear commercial routes out of the neighborhood without threading through tight residential cul-de-sacs. For a whole-house demo, that access efficiency matters: fewer truck passes, shorter cycle times, and less impact on neighbors — which matters when you are working under HOA scrutiny.
