The Beverly Hills Demolition Permit Process — Step by Step

Beverly Hills has a defined sequence that must be followed in order. Skipping or reordering steps causes delays. Here's how C&S works through it:

  1. Asbestos & Lead Survey (SCAQMD Rule 1403)

    SCAQMD Rule 1403 requires an asbestos and lead-containing materials survey for every structure before demolition permits are issued — regardless of when the structure was built. Beverly Hills Building & Safety requires the survey report as part of the permit application. C&S coordinates with a licensed environmental consultant to complete the survey efficiently. If regulated asbestos is found, a SCAQMD notification must be filed at least 10 business days before demolition begins — and abatement must be completed first.

  2. Utility Disconnection Letters

    Beverly Hills requires written confirmation of service termination from Southern California Edison (or LADWP, depending on the street), SoCalGas, and Beverly Hills Public Works / BWWD before the demolition permit is issued. C&S coordinates with each utility directly and assembles the documentation the City requires.

  3. Permit Application to Beverly Hills Building & Safety

    C&S prepares and submits a complete application including site plan, survey report, and utility disconnection letters. An incomplete application gets sent back — wasting weeks. We submit complete packages to move through plan check without delays.

  4. Permit Issued / Demolition Begins

    Once permits are in hand, C&S mobilizes. Typical demolition of a Beverly Hills estate runs 3–7 business days depending on structure size, foundation scope, and site access. Dust suppression and neighbor protection are standard — Beverly Hills code enforcement is active and neighbor expectations are high.

  5. Final Inspection and Permit Close

    Beverly Hills requires a final inspector walkthrough before the demolition permit is closed. This must happen before new construction permits can be issued on the lot. C&S coordinates the inspection and provides the documentation your general contractor needs to begin the new build permitting process.

Trousdale Estates, the Hills, and Flat-Lot Work — Different Conditions

Beverly Hills isn't a uniform environment. The conditions on a flat-lot estate in 90212 and a Trousdale Estates property off Loma Vista are completely different jobs — different equipment, different access strategy, different timeline.

The Beverly Hills Flats

Flat-lot estates typically have good street access and ample staging space. Standard equipment handles these efficiently. Most have older structures built in the 1950s–1970s with conventional wood-frame construction on concrete slab foundations. Relatively straightforward to permit and execute once the survey and utility documents are in order.

Trousdale Estates & Hillside Properties

Trousdale properties sit on steep lots with limited street frontage, narrow driveways, and close neighbor proximity. Equipment selection is critical — C&S uses compact reach excavators and carefully stages debris to avoid damaging retaining walls, adjacent landscaping, or neighboring structures. Every hillside project gets a dedicated access assessment during the site visit.

Benedict Canyon & Coldwater Canyon

Canyon properties have winding narrow roads that restrict heavy truck access. Debris hauling requires coordination — multiple smaller loads rather than single large hauls. C&S plans the haul schedule before mobilizing to avoid staging debris where it creates traffic or neighbor issues.

Neighbor Protection

In the hills, neighboring structures may be 15–20 feet away. C&S uses controlled demolition sequencing, dust suppression watering, and protective barriers throughout. Pre-demolition photo documentation of adjacent structures is standard practice — it protects both C&S and the property owner in any dispute.

Questions About Beverly Hills Teardowns

Does Beverly Hills use LA County for demolition permits?

No. Beverly Hills has its own independent Building & Safety Division. Demolition permits for Beverly Hills properties are issued by the City of Beverly Hills — not LA County or LADBS. Contractors who don't know this often prepare the wrong application package, which causes delays. C&S submits directly to Beverly Hills Building & Safety with the specific documentation their process requires.

What does the asbestos and lead survey cover?

Per SCAQMD Rule 1403, an asbestos and lead-containing materials survey is required for every structure before demolition — regardless of age or construction date. C&S coordinates with a licensed environmental inspector. Survey results must accompany the permit application. If regulated asbestos is identified, abatement must be completed before demolition and a 10-business-day SCAQMD notification must be filed.

How long does the full process take from start to cleared lot?

Most Beverly Hills whole house demolitions run 5–8 weeks from first call to cleared site. That includes permit processing (2–4 weeks at Beverly Hills Building & Safety), survey coordination (concurrent with permitting), and 3–7 business days of physical demolition depending on structure size and access. C&S builds this timeline into the initial project plan so your general contractor and architect can schedule around it.

Can C&S handle Trousdale properties specifically?

Yes. Trousdale Estates and canyon properties are part of our regular work in Beverly Hills. Steep lots, narrow driveways, and tight neighbor setbacks require equipment planning that flat-lot projects don't. We assess every hillside site during the free site visit and select equipment and haul strategy accordingly — no assumptions, no surprises.
