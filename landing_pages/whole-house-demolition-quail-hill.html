The Misconception That Costs Homeowners Time

Quail Hill was developed between the mid-1990s and the early 2000s — a period that followed the widespread phase-out of asbestos-containing materials in residential construction. Many property owners assume that a home built in 1999 or 2002 is automatically exempt from SCAQMD’s pre-demolition survey requirement. It is not. Under the South Coast Air Quality Management District’s current Rule 1403, the demolition of any structure requires a survey by a certified asbestos consultant prior to permit issuance, regardless of construction date. There is no age threshold that eliminates this step in the City of Irvine.

The practical impact is straightforward: if you begin the permit process at the City of Irvine Building & Safety Division before the SCAQMD survey is completed, your application will stall. C&S Demolition coordinates both simultaneously so neither becomes a bottleneck.

What SCAQMD Rule 1403 Actually Says No exemption exists for structures built after any specific year. Every demolition project in the South Coast Air Quality Management District — including all of Irvine — requires a state-certified asbestos consultant survey before work begins. A 2001-built Quail Hill home is as subject to this requirement as a 1975-built property in another city.

The Quail Hill HOA Step That Must Come First

Before you submit a demolition permit application to the City of Irvine, the Quail Hill Community Association’s Architectural Review Committee must approve the project. This is not a formality. The ARC reviews the scope of work, the proposed use of the cleared lot, impacts on neighboring parcels, and compliance with the community’s design guidelines. Quail Hill is a planned village with strict visual and land-use standards — the hillside character, trail adjacency, and open space buffers adjacent to many lots make the community association especially attentive to projects that alter the footprint of a structure.

Submitting to the City before receiving ARC approval is one of the most common errors we see from property owners attempting to manage demolition independently. The City of Irvine does not issue demolition permits for HOA-governed properties without confirmation of community association review, and the ARC process itself takes time. We help you prepare an ARC application package that anticipates the committee’s questions so approvals move efficiently.

Hillside Access on a Graded Lot

Quail Hill’s defining physical characteristic is its terrain. Homes in the community sit on stepped pad grading — benched cuts into the hillside that create level building pads, often separated by retaining walls and slope transitions. For whole house demolition, this terrain has real equipment implications. Heavy machinery used in demolition — excavators, skid steers, haul trucks — cannot always access the rear or side of a hillside lot directly from the street.

Many Quail Hill properties back up to the community’s extensive trail system and open space preserve. This adjacency eliminates the option of rear access entirely and often requires all equipment staging and debris loading to occur from the street frontage along Quail Hill Pkwy, Ridgeline, Shady Canyon Dr, or adjacent residential streets. C&S Demolition conducts a site-specific access assessment before mobilizing and coordinates with the City of Irvine on any required street use or haul route permits.

Utility Coordination in Quail Hill

Every whole house demolition in Quail Hill requires the disconnection and abandonment of utilities prior to demolition. Irvine Ranch Water District manages water and sewer service for the area. Southern California Edison serves electric, and SoCalGas handles natural gas. Each utility has its own disconnect and abandonment procedure, and none of them move at the same speed. We initiate all utility coordination on the front end of a project so that disconnections are completed — and utility company documentation is in hand — before demolition day.

The Sequence We Follow on Every Quail Hill Whole House Demo

Site Assessment & HOA Pre-Application Review

We walk the property, assess equipment access across your graded pad, identify slope and retaining wall constraints, and prepare the ARC application package. This step happens before any permit submission.

SCAQMD Survey Coordination

We coordinate with a certified asbestos consultant to complete the pre-demolition survey under Rule 1403. For 1995–2005 Quail Hill construction, regulated materials are unlikely, but the survey is required regardless and the results travel with your permit file.

City of Irvine Permit Application

With ARC approval and the SCAQMD survey complete, we submit the demolition permit application to the City of Irvine Building & Safety Division. We carry the paperwork and track the application through approval.

Utility Disconnections

IRWD, SCE, and SoCalGas are each contacted and disconnections are confirmed before mobilization. We obtain documentation for the permit file.

Demolition & Debris Hauling

Staged from street frontage when rear access is restricted, we demolish the structure, segregate recyclable concrete and wood, and haul all debris. Dust control is maintained throughout to protect adjacent trail users and neighboring properties.

Grading, Compaction & Final Inspection

The cleared pad is graded and compacted to City of Irvine specifications. Final inspection is scheduled and cleared before we demobilize.
