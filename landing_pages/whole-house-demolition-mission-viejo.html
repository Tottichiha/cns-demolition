Mission Viejo Was Built Quickly — and It Shows in the Materials

Mission Viejo is one of the largest master-planned communities in the country. The Irvine Company and Mission Viejo Company built thousands of homes here between 1966 and 1985, working fast to fill demand from Orange County's rapid postwar growth. The construction materials used during that window — specifically from the mid-1960s through 1978 — included asbestos-containing products that are now regulated under SCAQMD Rule 1403.

What that means practically: SCAQMD Rule 1403 requires an asbestos and lead-containing materials survey for all structures before demolition permits are issued — regardless of construction date. And in the older Mission Viejo neighborhoods — the homes along Jeronimo Road, in the original Casta del Sol tract, in the Aegean Hills area — positive findings are especially common. Popcorn ceiling texture is the most frequently found regulated material, but floor tile adhesive, duct tape, pipe insulation, and roofing felt from this era all regularly test positive.

If your Mission Viejo home was built before 1979, budget for asbestos abatement.

It doesn't always come up — but it comes up often enough in these neighborhoods that treating it as a contingency is wishful thinking. A positive asbestos finding means SCAQMD must be notified at minimum 10 business days before demolition begins, and abatement must be completed by a licensed contractor first. Plan for it. C&S helps you understand the realistic cost range before the survey so there are no surprises.

The permit itself comes from the City of Mission Viejo Building & Safety Division — not Orange County. Mission Viejo incorporated in 1988 and runs its own building department. The process is straightforward once all supporting documentation is assembled, but it must be done sequentially: survey first, permit application second, permit issued, then demo. Trying to shortcut that sequence creates problems.

The Demolition Process — Mission Viejo

  1. Initial Site Assessment & Survey CoordinationC&S walks the property and assesses the structure. SCAQMD Rule 1403 requires a certified asbestos and lead-containing materials survey for all structures before demolition permits are issued — regardless of construction date. We coordinate the survey with a licensed environmental consultant. Survey results drive the next steps, including whether abatement is required.
  2. Asbestos Abatement (If Required)If the survey finds regulated asbestos, SCAQMD must be notified a minimum of 10 business days before demolition begins. A licensed abatement contractor removes and disposes of the regulated material. C&S does not proceed with demolition until abatement is complete and clearance documentation is in hand.
  3. Utility DisconnectionsSoCalGas, SCE, Moulton Niguel Water District (the water utility for most of Mission Viejo), and sewer disconnection. Utility disconnection letters are required before the City will issue the demolition permit. C&S initiates these requests immediately after the project scope is confirmed.
  4. City of Mission Viejo Demolition PermitSubmitted to City Building & Safety with survey documentation, utility disconnection letters, and site plan. Straightforward permit review for residential demolition — typically 2–3 weeks. HOA notification submitted concurrently where required.
  5. Demolition & Site ClearanceStructure demolished and full debris removed. Concrete foundation disposition confirmed with owner before work starts — stays or goes depends on new construction plans. Site cleared and graded to handoff condition.

Frequently Asked Questions

My Mission Viejo home was built in 1974. How likely is asbestos?

Very likely somewhere in the structure. The 1970s were peak use of asbestos-containing building products in California residential construction. The most common findings in Mission Viejo homes from this era are popcorn ceiling texture (which was heavily used in 1970s production homes), 9x9 inch vinyl floor tile and its adhesive, insulation on heating ducts and pipes, and roofing felt. Not every survey finds regulated levels — but planning your project budget as if abatement is needed is the right approach. The cost of abatement surprises is worse than planning for it.

What water utility do I contact for disconnection in Mission Viejo?

Most of Mission Viejo is served by Moulton Niguel Water District for both water and sewer. Some northern areas near Laguna Hills are in Santa Margarita Water District territory. C&S identifies the correct utility district for your address at the project start and initiates disconnection coordination with the right agency. Using the wrong contact is a common reason for disconnection delays.

Does demolition require a permit from the City of Mission Viejo or Orange County?

City of Mission Viejo Building & Safety Division issues the permit. Mission Viejo incorporated as a city in 1988 and has run its own building department since. Orange County Building & Safety handles unincorporated areas of the county — not Mission Viejo addresses. This matters because permit requirements and fees differ between the City and the County.

Can you leave the foundation in place after demolition?

Yes, if new construction on the same footprint is planned and the architect or structural engineer specifies the existing foundation can be used. Foundation retention is confirmed in the demolition plan before work starts. If the foundation is being removed, C&S includes it in the scope and hauls all concrete debris off-site.
