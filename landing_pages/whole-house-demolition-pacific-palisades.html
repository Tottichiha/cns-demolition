Pacific Palisades After the January 2025 Fire

The January 2025 Palisades Fire fundamentally changed the demolition landscape in this part of Los Angeles. Hundreds of homes were destroyed across the Palisades Highlands, the Riviera, and the coastal neighborhoods along PCH. The scale of the damage created one of the largest concentrated residential demolition and rebuild situations in Southern California's history.

Two years later, the recovery is still actively underway. Some homeowners worked through the government-coordinated program; others chose private contractors for more control over timing and scope. Many are only now getting to the demolition phase as permits and insurance claims are resolved. C&S is working in Pacific Palisades and has navigated both the post-fire expedited LADBS pathways and the standard permit process for undamaged properties in the area.

Beyond fire recovery, Pacific Palisades also has an active standard teardown market independent of the fire. Older estates in Rustic Canyon, Huntington Palisades, and the upper Riviera are being cleared for new luxury construction — the same teardown-and-rebuild cycle happening across affluent Los Angeles neighborhoods.

Government Program vs. Private Contractor — What's the Difference?

For fire-damaged properties, homeowners have a choice. Understanding both options helps you decide which approach fits your situation.

Government Program (Army Corps / Cal OES)

  • Phase 2 debris removal at no direct cost to homeowner under eligible FEMA declarations
  • Scheduling controlled by government program — less flexibility
  • Timeline determined by program capacity, not your rebuild schedule
  • Standard scope defined by the program — less ability to customize
  • Coordination with rebuild GC may be more complex

Private Contractor (C&S Demolition)

  • Scheduling flexibility — you control when the work happens
  • Insurance-funded demolition — C&S provides claim documentation
  • Scope coordinated directly with your rebuild general contractor
  • Faster clearance when you need the site ready by a specific date
  • Direct communication and single point of contact throughout

C&S provides insurance-formatted invoicing, scope documentation, and pre/post site photos for claim submission. We coordinate directly with your adjuster and rebuild contractor.

Palisades Neighborhoods — Site Conditions and Access

The Riviera
Hillside neighborhood with ocean views and winding roads. Mix of flat-pad estates and steep-lot properties. Fire damage was concentrated in some sections of the Riviera. Equipment selection depends on individual lot grade and driveway access — assessed during site visit.
Castellammare
Coastal hillside area along PCH north of Malibu Colony. Many properties with dramatic bluff or ocean views, steep driveways, and limited staging. Some streets have restricted truck turning radius. C&S plans haul routes in advance for Castellammare projects.
Rustic Canyon
Tree-lined canyon neighborhood with larger lots and older estate properties. Relatively good access compared to steeper hillside areas. Many pre-war and mid-century structures — SCAQMD Rule 1403 asbestos survey required for all structures regardless of construction date. Active teardown market independent of the fire.
Huntington Palisades
Flat-to-gently-sloped neighborhood close to the Palisades Village. Some of the easiest access in Pacific Palisades. Predominantly post-war homes on good-sized lots. Strong teardown-and-rebuild demand, particularly for lots with ocean or canyon views.
Palisades Highlands
Upper elevation neighborhood above Sunset. Heavily affected by the 2025 fire. Steep hillside terrain, single-access roads on some streets, limited staging. C&S has worked in the Highlands and understands the access and haul challenges specific to this area.

LADBS Permit Process for Pacific Palisades

Pacific Palisades is part of the City of Los Angeles. LADBS handles all building permits.

For standard (non-fire) demolitions, the LADBS process involves asbestos and lead survey per SCAQMD Rule 1403 (required for all structures regardless of age), utility disconnections from LADWP, SoCalGas, and DWP water service, permit application submission, and demolition with final inspection. Standard processing runs approximately 3–5 weeks for complete applications.

For fire-damaged properties, LADBS has activated expedited permit pathways following the 2025 disaster declaration. The availability and terms of expedited programs change as the recovery evolves — C&S stays current on program status and advises clients on the fastest compliant pathway for their specific property.

SCAQMD Rule 1403 note: Rule 1403 applies to all structures regardless of construction date. Fire-damaged properties require testing for asbestos and lead-containing materials before demolition begins — fire debris may have higher concentrations of hazardous material than intact structures due to heat-accelerated release of bound materials.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who issues demolition permits in Pacific Palisades?

LADBS — the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety — issues all demolition permits in Pacific Palisades, which is part of the City of Los Angeles. For fire-damaged properties, expedited permit pathways may be available through LADBS or emergency programs. C&S monitors active program availability and advises clients on the current fastest pathway.

Should I use the government program or hire a private contractor?

The government program (Army Corps under Cal OES) is available at no direct cost for qualifying fire-damaged properties but offers less scheduling control. Private contractors like C&S give you control over timing, better coordination with your rebuild GC, and insurance claim documentation support. Many homeowners choose private demolition to start their rebuild sooner and maintain a predictable site clearance date.

Does SCAQMD Rule 1403 apply to my fire-damaged property?

Yes. SCAQMD Rule 1403 applies to all structures before demolition, including fire-damaged properties — regardless of construction date or fire damage extent. Fire debris may contain concentrated hazardous materials that require specialized testing and disposal documentation beyond standard demolition waste protocols. C&S coordinates with licensed environmental consultants for fire-specific hazmat assessment and disposal.
