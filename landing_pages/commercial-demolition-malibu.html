Commercial Demo in a Coastal Jurisdiction — What That Means for Your Project

Malibu's commercial district runs primarily along Pacific Coast Highway — a narrow band of retail, dining, and service businesses operating within one of California's most tightly regulated coastal environments. Any commercial demolition here navigates two distinct regulatory frameworks: LA County Building & Safety (which handles Malibu's building permits under a contractual arrangement) and the Coastal Act, which gives the City of Malibu's planning department review authority over work in the Coastal Zone.

For interior tenant improvement demolition, this layered review usually doesn't extend project timelines significantly. For full structure teardowns — particularly on PCH-adjacent parcels — Coastal Development Permit requirements typically apply and must be factored into the project schedule from the start.

C&S works with property owners, tenants, and general contractors on commercial demo in Malibu's retail centers and along the PCH corridor. We assess the regulatory path, coordinate permits, and sequence the demo work so it doesn't become the bottleneck in your project timeline.

LA County Permit Process — Commercial Demo in Malibu

  1. Asbestos & Lead Survey (SCAQMD Rule 1403)Required before any demolition permit is issued — regardless of structure age. Commercial structures in Malibu's older retail centers along PCH have a higher likelihood of regulated materials than newer builds. C&S coordinates with licensed environmental consultants. If regulated asbestos is found, 10-business-day SCAQMD notification and abatement precede demolition.
  2. Utility Disconnection CoordinationGas (SoCalGas), electric (SCE), water, and sewer disconnection letters required for full structure teardowns. For TI interior demos, individual utility service to the tenant space must be isolated and confirmed off before demo begins — separate from the building's main service.
  3. Coastal Zone DeterminationCity of Malibu's planning department determines whether the scope triggers Coastal Development Permit review. TI interior demolition typically does not. Full structure teardown on Coastal Zone parcels typically does. C&S identifies the likely determination at the site visit stage.
  4. LA County Permit ApplicationSubmitted to LA County Building & Safety with all required supporting documentation. C&S prepares and submits the complete application package — survey report, utility letters, and site plan identifying the demo scope.
  5. Post-Demo Inspection & Permit CloseRequired before new construction permits are issued for the same footprint. C&S schedules and is present for the inspector walkthrough at job completion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who issues commercial demolition permits in Malibu?

LA County Building & Safety issues all building and demolition permits for structures in Malibu under a contractual services agreement with the City. Coastal Zone review is a separate determination made by the City of Malibu's Community Development department — that process runs in parallel to the LA County permit application for projects that trigger it.

How long does commercial demo permitting take in Malibu?

TI interior demo that doesn't require a separate demolition permit (covered under the remodel permit) can begin once the remodel permit is issued. Standalone demolition permits through LA County typically take 2–4 weeks when the application is complete. If asbestos is found, add at minimum 10 business days for SCAQMD notification. If a CDP is required, Coastal Zone review timelines vary based on the scope and any discretionary review triggers.

Can C&S work within active commercial centers without disrupting other tenants?

Yes — multi-tenant commercial work in Malibu's retail centers requires coordinating work hours, protecting shared walls and ceilings, managing dust containment, and routing debris removal to minimize impact on neighboring businesses. C&S reviews site conditions and shared structure configuration with the property manager before scheduling starts.

What does the asbestos survey cover for commercial buildings?

SCAQMD Rule 1403 requires a complete asbestos and lead-containing materials survey for all commercial structures regardless of construction date. The survey covers building materials that will be disturbed during demolition — surfacing materials, thermal system insulation, miscellaneous ACMs. C&S coordinates the survey with a licensed inspector and uses the results to inform the permit application and the demolition plan.
