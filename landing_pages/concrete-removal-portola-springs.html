The Hillside Factor

Most concrete removal guides assume flat suburban lots. Portola Springs does not have flat suburban lots. The village was carved into the foothills at the eastern edge of Irvine, and the grading work that created the residential pads was substantial. What that means on the ground is that driveways slope — often significantly — from the street to the garage; patio slabs at the rear of the home may sit above retaining walls with drainage systems running at their base; side-yard concrete pads may be at different elevations from the main lot surface, separated by small retaining structures.

This matters for concrete removal in a very practical way. Breaking up and removing a patio slab that sits on top of a slope drainage system requires knowing where that drainage runs before the first jackhammer strike. A drainage channel, a French drain, or a perforated pipe below a slab can be destroyed in minutes by a crew that doesn’t know it’s there. Repairing slope drainage infrastructure after the fact is a separate, expensive project that the homeowner did not budget for and the HOA will take notice of.

C&S Demolition walks every Portola Springs site before mobilizing. We look at the grading, the retaining wall interfaces, and the drainage pathway on each concrete removal project. We ask your landscaper or the original builder’s drainage plan if it’s available. We break concrete by hand-tool first at the edges near drainage features before bringing in mechanical equipment.

On retaining walls: Many Portola Springs homes have retaining walls along rear and side property lines that were built as part of the original lot grading by Toll Brothers, Shea Homes, or the other builders who developed this village. Concrete flatwork — patio slabs, side-yard paths, planter borders — often abuts or integrates with those retaining walls. Removing the concrete without disturbing the wall’s footing or the soil retention behind it requires careful sequencing. We identify the footing interface before breaking the adjacent slab, and we do not use heavy mechanical equipment within the influence zone of a retaining wall without first evaluating the risk.

What We Remove in Portola Springs

Sloped Driveways

Portola Springs driveways typically run from street grade down to an attached garage set at the home’s finished floor elevation. The approach slope is usually 10–20%. Removal requires controlling debris on the slope and protecting the garage apron and street curb during breakout.

Rear Patio Slabs

Rear patios in this community often sit on engineered fill with slope drainage at the perimeter. We identify drainage outlet locations before breaking the slab and work away from drainage infrastructure.

Side-Yard Paths & Pads

Narrow side-yard concrete paths with limited equipment access are common. We use electric demolition hammers where tracked equipment cannot safely operate, then hand-carry debris to the staging area.

Retaining Wall Footings

When a retaining wall itself needs to come down as part of a landscape redesign, the footing removal must be sequenced carefully to avoid undermining adjacent grading. We assess slope stability before this work begins.

Pool Decking

Concrete pool decking in Portola Springs homes often wraps partially around hillside pools with grade changes. Equipment access varies; we assess each site for the right removal method.

Garage Aprons & Approach Slabs

The transition slab between the driveway and the garage floor is a high-traffic, structurally critical area. Removal is sequenced to leave the garage threshold intact unless the scope calls for its removal.

The HOA and Permits

Concrete removal in Portola Springs sits at an interesting intersection of HOA oversight and City of Irvine permitting. The Portola Springs Community Association’s ARC governs exterior modifications, and replacing a driveway or patio surface is generally considered an exterior modification that requires ARC submittal. The CC&Rs in this village specify approved materials, colors, and finishes for hardscape — among the most detailed hardscape rules in Irvine.

For removal-only projects with no replacement component, the ARC trigger is lower, but the City of Irvine may still require a grading permit if the removal disturbs the lot’s engineered grading or drainage infrastructure. IRWD has jurisdiction over anything that touches the site’s water supply or drainage connections. We identify the permit requirement at the scoping stage so there are no surprises after equipment is on site.

The SCAQMD Rule 1403 survey applies when concrete removal is part of a broader demolition scope that includes a structure. For standalone concrete flatwork removal, the SCAQMD requirements are different — we clarify applicability based on your specific project at the outset.
