Newport Beach Demolition Permit Process

Newport Beach is governed by its own Community Development Department — not Orange County. The permit process is generally more efficient than LA County, but the coastal zone adds a step for many properties:

  1. Asbestos & Lead Survey — SCAQMD Rule 1403

    Required for all structures before permit issuance, regardless of construction date. Newport Beach requires the survey report as part of the demolition permit application. C&S coordinates with licensed environmental consultants to get the survey completed without delaying the permit timeline.

  2. Coastal Zone / CDP Determination

    Properties within Newport Beach's Local Coastal Program area may require a Coastal Development Permit in addition to the standard demolition permit. Newport Beach processes most CDPs locally under its certified LCP — only "appealable area" properties go to the California Coastal Commission. Your land use consultant determines which path applies before any applications are filed.

  3. Utility Disconnections

    Written confirmation of service termination from Southern California Edison, SoCalGas, and City of Newport Beach utilities (water/sewer) must be assembled before permit submission. C&S coordinates each utility directly and documents the disconnections.

  4. Permit Submission to Newport Beach Community Development

    C&S submits a complete application package. Newport Beach typically issues demolition permits in 2–3 weeks for straightforward applications — faster than most LA County jurisdictions. Coastal zone applications may add 2–4 weeks.

  5. Demolition and Debris Removal

    Demolition proceeds per permit. C&S removes all debris, grades the lot, and leaves the site clean for your grading contractor or civil engineer. Final site photos and permit closeout documentation provided.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who issues demolition permits in Newport Beach?

The City of Newport Beach Community Development Department issues demolition permits — not Orange County. Newport Beach is an independent city with its own permit process. C&S submits directly to the City with the specific documentation Newport Beach requires, which differs from what Orange County and LA County applications need.

Does the Coastal Zone affect my Newport Beach teardown?

Potentially, yes. Most of Newport Beach's coastal neighborhoods fall within the California Coastal Zone, which means a Coastal Development Permit may be required in addition to the standard demolition permit. Newport Beach handles most CDPs locally through its certified Local Coastal Program. Only properties in the appealable area go to the California Coastal Commission. C&S works with your land use consultant on this determination.

How does C&S handle the constraints of Balboa Island?

Balboa Island requires project-specific planning that other Newport Beach neighborhoods don't. Narrow streets limit equipment size and truck access. Many lots are 2,500–3,500 sq ft with homes close to or at property lines. C&S uses compact excavators, stages debris carefully within the available space, and schedules haul pickups to avoid conflicts with island traffic patterns. Every island project gets an individual site assessment before any work is planned.

What does SCAQMD Rule 1403 require?

SCAQMD Rule 1403 requires an asbestos and lead-containing materials survey for every structure before demolition permits are issued — regardless of when the structure was built. C&S coordinates with a licensed environmental inspector and assembles the survey documentation for the permit application. If regulated asbestos is found, a 10-business-day SCAQMD notification is required before demo begins, and abatement must be completed first.
