The ARC Approval Is the Real Starting Line

Most homeowners think demolition begins with calling the city. In Portola Springs, it begins with the Portola Springs Community Association’s Architectural Review Committee. The ARC submittal package for any exterior modification — and full demolition is about as exterior as it gets — requires architectural drawings, material specifications, and for rebuilds, color samples and elevation plans. The CC&Rs in this village are more granular than almost any other community in Irvine. Approval timelines typically run 30–45 days from submission, which means the ARC process needs to begin before a demolition contractor even mobilizes equipment.

C&S Demolition works alongside your architect or project manager to provide the documentation the ARC needs from the demolition side: scope of work, site control plan, debris removal logistics, and utility disconnect sequencing. Getting the ARC package right the first time avoids the resubmission cycle that can push your entire project timeline back months.

Once ARC approval is in hand, we coordinate the City of Irvine Building & Safety demolition permit in parallel with any utility disconnects through SCE, SoCalGas, and IRWD. These three agencies operate on independent timelines and need to be managed as a sequence, not an afterthought.

Why Portola Springs Homes Are Different

Portola Springs was developed between approximately 2005 and 2018 — Irvine’s final major planned village. The builders who worked this community — Toll Brothers, William Lyon Homes, KB Homes, Shea Homes — built some of Irvine’s largest production homes, many in the 3,000–5,000 square foot range. These are not small infill teardowns. They are two-story structures with attached two-car garages, complex rooflines, and hillside foundations that sit on graded pads with slope drainage behind them.

The hillside character of Portola Springs — bordering Foothill Ranch and Trabuco Canyon in the foothills of Irvine — means that grading and drainage are live concerns during demolition. Retaining walls along side yards and rear slopes are common. Vibration from heavy equipment can shift drainage infrastructure if a crew isn’t paying attention. We assess slope features before mobilizing and protect existing drainage during the demo sequence.

The good news is that Portola Springs homes carry the lowest asbestos risk of any Irvine village precisely because of their 2005+ construction dates. California phased out most regulated asbestos-containing materials in building products well before this community was built. That said, the SCAQMD survey is non-optional under current Rule 1403 — the regulation has no construction-date exemption, and failure to obtain one before demolition is a violation that stops the project and carries significant fines. We arrange the survey as a standard part of every engagement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need HOA approval before getting a city demolition permit?

Yes. In Portola Springs, the Portola Springs Community Association’s ARC must approve any significant exterior modification before the City of Irvine will issue a demolition permit. The ARC approval letter becomes part of the city permit application package. Trying to get the city permit first leads to a rejected application.

My house was built in 2011. Do I still need an asbestos survey?

Yes. SCAQMD Rule 1403 applies to all demolition projects in the South Coast Air Basin regardless of construction date. There is no exemption for post-2000 construction. The survey must be completed by a certified asbestos consultant before demolition begins, and results must be filed with SCAQMD. We arrange this for every project.

How long does the full process take from first call to demolition day?

For a Portola Springs whole-house demolition, you should budget 8–14 weeks from first engagement to mobilization. The ARC review alone runs 30–45 days. Utility disconnects, SCAQMD notification, and the City of Irvine permit add additional time. We work all tracks simultaneously to compress the overall timeline.

Will the demo crew damage the drainage and grading on my hillside lot?

We assess every site for grading features, retaining walls, and slope drainage before mobilizing. Portola Springs lots commonly have rear and side-yard slope drainage that runs beneath or adjacent to structures. Our crew marks and protects these systems during demolition.

Does C&S Demolition handle the ARC submittal on my behalf?

We prepare the demolition-specific documentation the ARC requires — scope of work, site control plan, equipment staging, and debris logistics. Architectural drawings for the rebuild must come from a licensed architect, but we coordinate closely to ensure our demolition documents align with what the ARC expects.
