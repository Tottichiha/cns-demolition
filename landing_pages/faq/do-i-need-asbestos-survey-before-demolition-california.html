California Demolition FAQ

Do I Need an Asbestos Survey Before Demolition in California?

Short answer: yes. Here is what the law requires, what it costs, and what to do if asbestos is found.

Short Answer
Yes — California law requires a signed asbestos survey report from a Cal/OSHA-certified inspector before you can demolish any structure 100 square feet or larger. This applies statewide, regardless of when your building was constructed.

The Two Laws That Make This Mandatory

Two separate legal frameworks require asbestos surveys before demolition in California, and both apply to most residential projects:

1. California Health and Safety Code §19827.5

This state statute requires demolition permit applicants to either submit asbestos notifications to the appropriate air quality management district or provide a written declaration that the requirements do not apply. Because full demolitions nearly always exceed the applicable thresholds, you generally cannot claim the exemption — you must survey.

2. SCAQMD Rule 1403 (Southern California)

For projects in Los Angeles, Orange, Riverside, and San Bernardino counties, SCAQMD Rule 1403 is the operative regulation. It mandates a pre-project asbestos survey for all demolition and renovation projects involving structures of 100 square feet or more. Unlike some older regulations, Rule 1403 has no age-of-building exemption — even a building constructed last year must be surveyed.

Important: The survey is required before permit issuance, not just before demolition work starts. Your permit application package typically must include the survey report. Submitting without it will result in an incomplete application and a longer permit timeline.

What the Survey Involves

A pre-demolition asbestos survey is a physical inspection and sampling process — not a records review or visual-only walkthrough. The inspector will:

  • Visually inspect all accessible building materials that could contain asbestos
  • Physically collect bulk samples from suspected materials (ceiling texture, floor tile, pipe wrap, etc.)
  • Send samples to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy (PLM) analysis
  • Issue a signed written report identifying all asbestos-containing materials (ACMs), their locations, and estimated quantities

For a full demolition, this is a comprehensive survey — not the limited renovation survey that only covers the area being disturbed.

What Happens If Asbestos Is Found?

Finding asbestos does not automatically derail your project — it just adds steps. Here is the sequence:

  • The inspector identifies regulated asbestos-containing materials in the report
  • You (or your contractor) notify SCAQMD using the official notification form, at least 10 business days before abatement begins
  • A California-licensed asbestos abatement contractor removes and properly disposes of all ACMs under strict containment protocols
  • A final clearance air test may be required depending on the abatement scope
  • Once abatement is certified complete, demolition can proceed

The 10-business-day SCAQMD notification window is fixed by law — it cannot be shortened by paying a fee or requesting an expedite. Plan accordingly.

What About Lead Paint?

The asbestos survey does not cover lead-based paint. If your structure was built before 1978, lead paint testing is separately required for contractor protection under Cal/OSHA regulations. Pre-1940 homes almost universally have lead paint in multiple layers. Most experienced demolition contractors address both in the pre-project phase to avoid disrupting work mid-project.
