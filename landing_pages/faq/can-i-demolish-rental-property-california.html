California Demolition FAQ

Can I Demolish a Rental Property in California?

Demolishing a rental unit in a rent-control city requires more than a building permit. Here is what you actually need to do.

Demolishing rental property in California is legal, but in cities with Rent Stabilization Ordinances (RSO), it triggers a separate layer of requirements designed to protect tenants from being displaced without compensation. Getting this wrong can block your demolition permit entirely. Here is a complete guide to what is required.

Does Your Property Fall Under Rent Control?

California's Costa-Hawkins Rental Housing Act limits rent control to certain older properties, but local RSO protections can be broader. Your rental units are likely subject to rent control demolition requirements if the property is located in:

  • Los Angeles (City)
  • Santa Monica
  • West Hollywood
  • Beverly Hills
  • Culver City
  • Inglewood
  • East Palo Alto
  • Hayward
  • Oakland
  • San Jose
  • Berkeley
  • Los Gatos

Even if your property is not in a city with rent control, California's statewide AB 1482 (Tenant Protection Act of 2019) provides just-cause eviction protections for most multi-unit housing built more than 15 years ago. Consult a real estate attorney before beginning the eviction process for any rental demolition.

The Process for Demolishing a Rent-Controlled Rental in Los Angeles

Critical sequence: The Los Angeles Housing Department (LAHD) will not issue a clearance letter until tenant relocation obligations are met. Building and Safety will not issue a demolition permit without that LAHD clearance letter. The order of operations matters — do not start eviction proceedings without legal advice.
  1. Confirm RSO coverage: Verify with LAHD (or your city's equivalent housing department) that the unit is covered by the RSO. Not all rental units are — single-family homes and condos are often exempt, though statewide protections may still apply.
  2. Engage a real estate attorney: RSO demolition evictions are technical. The wrong notice, the wrong notice period, or failure to pay relocation assistance correctly can void the eviction and expose you to liability. An attorney familiar with local RSO law is essential.
  3. Serve proper notice of termination: The notice must state that the reason for termination is demolition for new construction or substantial rehabilitation. The notice period varies — typically 120 days for at-fault tenants, longer for long-term tenants.
  4. Pay tenant relocation assistance: Relocation assistance must be paid within 15 days of serving the termination notice. Amounts depend on unit size, tenant classification (Qualified tenants receive more), and whether tenants are low-income. LAHD publishes current relocation amounts.
  5. Obtain LAHD clearance letter: Once relocation obligations are satisfied, LAHD issues a written clearance to the Department of Building and Safety confirming compliance.
  6. Proceed with standard demolition permit: With LAHD clearance in hand, you can submit to Building and Safety along with the standard permit package (asbestos survey, utility clearances, etc.).

What Are Qualified Tenants?

Under the Los Angeles RSO, Qualified Tenants receive higher relocation assistance payments. This category includes:

  • Tenants 62 years of age or older
  • Tenants with a disability as defined under the Fair Employment and Housing Act
  • Tenants with one or more minor dependent children
  • Tenants who have resided in the unit for 10 or more years

Misclassifying a Qualified Tenant or underpaying relocation assistance gives LAHD grounds to withhold clearance and gives the tenant grounds to challenge the eviction and seek damages.

What About Single-Family Rentals?

Single-family homes rented to tenants occupy a different legal space. Under the Los Angeles RSO, single-family homes have historically been exempt from rent stabilization — but they may still be covered by statewide just-cause eviction protections under AB 1482. The key question is whether the owner has provided proper documentation of exemption and whether the eviction follows the required process. Do not assume a single-family rental is exempt without verifying with counsel.

Practical note: RSO-covered demolitions typically add 3 to 6 months to the overall project timeline — in addition to the standard 4–8 week permit process. Budget your financing timeline accordingly.
