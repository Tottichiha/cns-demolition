Southern California Demolition Regulations

What Is SCAQMD Rule 1403?

The asbestos regulation that applies to every demolition and renovation project in Southern California — and what it means for your project.

If you are planning to demolish or renovate a structure in Los Angeles, Orange, Riverside, or San Bernardino County, you will encounter SCAQMD Rule 1403 before your project gets off the ground. It is one of the most consequential environmental regulations affecting demolition work in Southern California, and many property owners first hear about it from their contractor — or worse, from an inspector.

What Rule 1403 Requires

Rule 1403, enforced by the South Coast Air Quality Management District (SCAQMD), was designed to prevent asbestos fibers from becoming airborne during demolition and renovation. Asbestos is a known human carcinogen; even brief exposure to disturbed fibers carries serious health risks.

The rule has three main requirements:

  1. Pre-project asbestos survey — A Cal/OSHA-certified asbestos consultant must inspect and physically sample the structure before any demolition or renovation work begins. This applies to all structures 100 square feet or larger, regardless of construction date.
  2. SCAQMD notification — You must notify SCAQMD of any demolition project using the official notification form, even if no asbestos was found. Notification must be submitted at least 14 calendar days before starting demolition.
  3. Abatement notification — If regulated asbestos-containing materials are identified, a separate notice to SCAQMD is required at least 10 business days before abatement begins. Only a licensed asbestos abatement contractor may perform the removal.

Does Rule 1403 Apply to New or Newer Buildings?

Yes — and this surprises many property owners. There is no construction date exemption under the current Rule 1403. Whether your house was built in 1925 or 2005, a pre-project asbestos survey is required before any demolition. The only narrow exception is renovation of a residential single-unit dwelling where less than 100 square feet of intact, undamaged material is disturbed — a threshold a full-home demo never meets.

Why does this matter for newer buildings? Asbestos-containing materials were widely used in construction through the late 1970s, but some products remained in use into the 1980s. Certain floor tiles, duct insulation, and roofing mastics contained asbestos well past 1980. A survey is the only way to know for certain.

What Asbestos-Containing Materials Are Commonly Found?

Experienced inspectors routinely find regulated materials in:

  • Popcorn (acoustic spray) ceilings — especially in homes built before 1980
  • 9×9 inch floor tiles and their mastic adhesive
  • Pipe and duct wrap insulation
  • Roofing felt and shingles (certain products)
  • Textured wall and ceiling plaster (pre-1940 homes nearly universally)
  • Vermiculite attic insulation
  • Window caulking and glazing compounds

Who Must Conduct the Survey?

Rule 1403 requires the survey be performed by a Cal/OSHA-certified asbestos inspector or an AHERA Building Inspector with a valid certificate from a Cal/OSHA-approved training course. The written report must be signed by the inspector and kept on-site during demolition. Unlicensed inspections — including DIY sampling — do not satisfy the rule.

What Happens If You Skip It?

Penalties are severe. Violations of Rule 1403 carry fines of up to $20,000 per day per violation. If asbestos is released into the environment due to a contractor's negligence — including failure to survey — criminal charges and jail time are possible. Stop-work orders are common when inspectors find demolition proceeding without a compliant survey on file.

Beyond the regulatory penalties, property owners can face civil liability from neighboring residents if asbestos fibers migrate off-site during demolition. The survey is not optional, and its cost — typically $500 to $1,500 for a single-family residence — is trivial compared to the legal exposure of skipping it.
