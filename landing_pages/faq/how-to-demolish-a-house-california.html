Step-by-Step Guide

How to Demolish a House in California

Every phase of a legal California house demolition — from the first phone call to a cleared, graded lot.

Demolishing a house in California is more involved than in many other states. Environmental regulations, mandatory surveys, utility clearances, and a multi-agency permit process mean the work itself is often the fastest part of the project. Here is a realistic, phase-by-phase guide to how it actually works.

The Six Phases of a California House Demolition

1

Hire a CSLB-Licensed Demolition Contractor

Week 1

California requires all demolition contractors to hold a valid Contractors State License Board (CSLB) license. Verify any contractor you consider at cslb.ca.gov before signing a contract. Look for active license status, workers' compensation insurance, and a general liability policy with you named as additional insured.

Get a written scope of work — not just a price — that specifies what is and is not included: permit, survey, abatement coordination, foundation removal, and debris hauling.

2

Asbestos and Lead Survey

Weeks 1–2

A Cal/OSHA-certified asbestos inspector must physically inspect and sample the structure before any permit can be issued. This is required under SCAQMD Rule 1403 for all structures 100 square feet or larger — no age exemption applies.

  • Survey report is signed by the inspector and required for the permit package
  • If regulated asbestos is found, abatement must occur before demolition
  • Lead paint testing (separate from asbestos) is standard for pre-1978 buildings
  • Pre-1940 homes almost universally have both lead and asbestos — budget for abatement
3

Utility Disconnections

Weeks 1–3 (concurrent)

All utilities must be formally disconnected by their respective providers before the city will issue a demolition permit. Start these calls the same week you engage a contractor — utility providers often have their own scheduling backlogs.

  • Natural gas: Contact SoCalGas to terminate service and cap the line. Request written confirmation.
  • Electricity: Contact SCE (or your city's electric utility) to pull the meter and disconnect service.
  • Water and sewer: Contact the city water department to cap off service at the main.
  • Cable/telecom: Notify providers to remove drop lines before demo day.
4

Demolition Permit Application and Plan Check

Weeks 2–7

Submit the permit package to your city's Building and Safety department. Required items typically include:

  • Completed demolition permit application
  • Signed asbestos survey report
  • Utility disconnection clearance letters
  • Site plan showing structure location on the lot
  • Contractor's CSLB license number and insurance certificates

Plan check typically takes 2–6 weeks. Expedited plan check (paid) is available at most cities and can cut this significantly. In rent-control cities (Los Angeles, Santa Monica, West Hollywood, etc.), a housing department clearance letter is also required for rental units.

5

Asbestos Abatement (If Required)

Variable — 1 to 3 weeks

If regulated asbestos-containing materials were found, a licensed abatement contractor removes them under strict containment protocols. SCAQMD must be notified at least 10 business days before abatement begins — this window cannot be shortened.

Common materials requiring abatement in Southern California homes: popcorn ceiling texture, 9×9 vinyl floor tile and mastic, duct and pipe wrap insulation, and roofing felt.

6

Demolition, Debris Removal, and Grading

Demo day: 1–3 days

With permit in hand and abatement complete, the actual demolition is typically the fastest phase. An excavator knocks down the structure in one to two days, with debris sorted for recycling and hauling over another day or two. The lot is then rough-graded and inspected.

California has construction and demolition (C&D) debris diversion requirements under AB 939 — most cities require a certain percentage of demo debris to be recycled or diverted from landfill. Your contractor should provide a C&D debris diversion report with disposal receipts.

Realistic total timeline: From initial contractor call to cleared, graded lot: 6 to 12 weeks for a typical single-family home. The active demolition takes 2–4 days — the permit and pre-demo compliance phase is where the calendar time goes.

What Happens to the Lot After Demolition?

Once the structure is down and debris is removed, the lot is rough-graded by the contractor to a stable, drainable surface. If you plan to build immediately, your general contractor or grading contractor will take over for finish grading and foundation work. If the lot will sit vacant, check with your city about requirements for dust control, erosion prevention, and fencing.
