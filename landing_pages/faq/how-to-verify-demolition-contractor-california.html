How to Verify a Demolition Contractor's License in California

Three minutes on cslb.ca.gov can save you from an enormous headache. Here is exactly what to check.

In California, any contractor performing demolition work on a structure must hold a valid CSLB (Contractors State License Board) license. Hiring an unlicensed demolition contractor is one of the most costly mistakes a property owner can make — permits may be voided, you lose all CSLB consumer protections, and you take on personal liability for any injuries on-site. Verifying a license takes three minutes and costs nothing.

How to Check a CSLB License — Step by Step

Verify Any Contractor at cslb.ca.gov

  1. Go to www.cslb.ca.gov
  2. Click "Check a License" in the main navigation
  3. Enter the contractor's license number OR business name
  4. Review the results — confirm Active status, correct classifications, and insurance on file

The CSLB database is updated daily. If a contractor's license is suspended, expired, or revoked, it will show immediately.

What a Valid License Record Should Show

  • Status: Active — Any other status (Suspended, Expired, Revoked, Inactive) means the contractor cannot legally perform work.
  • Classification includes demolition work — Look for C-21 (Building Moving/Demolition), A (General Engineering), or B (General Building).
  • Workers' Compensation Insurance: On File — If it says "Exempt," ask the contractor to explain. Sole proprietors with no employees can be exempt — but a demolition company with a crew should have WC on file.
  • Bond: On File — California requires a $25,000 contractor's bond. Confirm it is current.
  • No disciplinary actions — The CSLB record shows any citations, fines, or formal accusations. A single minor citation is not disqualifying; a pattern of violations or a formal accusation is a serious red flag.
  • Avoid: "Pending" license status — A contractor with a pending (not yet issued) license cannot legally perform work.
  • Avoid: License number that does not match the business name on the contract — Verify that the license belongs to the entity you are signing a contract with, not a related company.

What CSLB License Classification Covers Demolition?

California has 44 contractor license classifications. For demolition work, the most relevant are:

  • C-21 — Building Moving/Demolition: The specialty classification specifically for demolition work. A C-21 contractor can perform demolition without needing additional classifications.
  • A — General Engineering Contractor: Can perform demolition as part of a broader construction or civil engineering project.
  • B — General Building Contractor: Can perform demolition of structures when it is part of a larger building project. Some B-licensed contractors specialize in demo.

Red Flags to Watch For When Getting Bids

Demands full cash payment upfront. California law limits deposits to 10% of the contract price or $1,000, whichever is less. Anyone demanding full payment before work starts is either unlicensed or operating illegally.
Cannot provide a certificate of insurance on the spot. Any legitimate demolition contractor can email you a COI from their broker within an hour. No insurance documentation = no work.
Skips the asbestos survey and says it is not needed. Under SCAQMD Rule 1403, surveys are required for all structures 100 sq ft or larger. A contractor who waves this off is either uninformed or cutting corners.
Does not pull a permit. "We can skip the permit and save you money" is a phrase that costs property owners far more in the long run — fines, stop-work orders, and title problems when you try to sell or build.
