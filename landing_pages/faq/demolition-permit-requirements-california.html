California Demolition FAQ

Demolition Permit Requirements in California

A complete checklist of what your permit application must include — and what will get it kicked back.

Demolition permit requirements in California vary slightly by city, but the core documents are consistent statewide. Submitting an incomplete package is the single most common reason applications are delayed — the city puts it in a corrections queue, which can cost weeks. Here is what to have ready before you submit.

Core Permit Application Requirements

  • Completed Demolition Permit Application Form Available from your city's Building and Safety public counter or website. Must include property address, APN, owner name, and scope of work description.
  • Signed Asbestos Survey Report Required under SCAQMD Rule 1403 and California Health and Safety Code §19827.5. Must be signed by a Cal/OSHA-certified asbestos inspector. The report must cover the entire structure being demolished.
  • Utility Disconnection Clearances Written confirmation from SoCalGas (or local gas utility), SCE or municipal electric, and city water that service has been terminated and capped. Some cities also require telecom provider confirmation.
  • Site Plan A simple site plan showing the location of the structure on the lot, setbacks from property lines, and location of adjacent structures. Does not need to be an engineered drawing for most residential demos.
  • Contractor CSLB License Number The demolition contractor must hold a valid, active California Contractors State License. Verify at cslb.ca.gov. The license number must appear on the application. Homeowner-owner-builder permits are available in some cases for single-family residences.
  • Proof of Insurance Certificate of general liability insurance and workers' compensation, with the city typically named as additional insured. Minimum coverage amounts vary by city — usually $1M general liability.
  • Permit Fee Payment Demo permit fees typically range from $300 to $2,000 for residential structures depending on city fee schedules and structure valuation. Fee schedules are publicly posted on city Building and Safety websites.

Additional Requirements for Specific Situations

Rent-Controlled Rental Units

If the structure being demolished is a rental unit covered by a Rent Stabilization Ordinance (RSO) — including properties in Los Angeles, Santa Monica, West Hollywood, Culver City, Inglewood, and other cities — additional clearances are required before Building and Safety will issue a demo permit:

  • Written clearance from the city's Housing Department (LAHD in Los Angeles) confirming that the owner has complied with tenant relocation assistance requirements
  • Proof of required relocation payments to all displaced tenants
  • In some cities, a separate Rent Control Board demolition permit

Historic or Potentially Historic Structures

If your property is in a designated historic district, listed on a local or state historic register, or identified as a potential historic resource, a historic review is required before the demo permit can be issued. This can add weeks or months to the timeline. Contact your city's Planning Department before starting if you have any reason to believe the structure may qualify.

Slopes, Hillsides, and Unstable Soils

Properties on slopes, near retaining walls, or in known liquefaction or landslide zones may require a geotechnical (soils) report demonstrating that demolition will not destabilize adjacent properties.

Which Agency Issues the Permit?

Demolition permits in California are issued by your local city's Building and Safety department — not the county (unless you are in an unincorporated area), and not SCAQMD. SCAQMD handles the air quality notification requirement, which is separate from the building permit. You need both to be in compliance.

Common point of confusion: The SCAQMD notification for demolition (even when no asbestos is found) is required to be submitted at least 14 calendar days before demolition begins. This is a separate filing from the building permit and goes directly to SCAQMD — not to the city. Your contractor should handle both.

What Gets Applications Kicked Back

  • Missing or unsigned asbestos survey (most common)
  • Utility clearances not yet received — start utility calls early
  • Contractor listed on application has an expired or inactive CSLB license
  • Site plan does not show all structures on the parcel
  • Property is a rental unit without housing department clearance
  • Permit fee check/payment missing or incorrect amount
Penalty reminder: Demolishing without a permit in California can result in fines of $1,000 to $5,000 per violation, stop-work orders, and mandatory retroactive inspections that can be expensive and time-consuming. "I didn't know I needed a permit" is not a valid defense.
