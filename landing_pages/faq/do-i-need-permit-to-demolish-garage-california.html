Do I Need a Permit to Demolish a Garage in California?

What the permit process involves, how California ADU laws affect garage demolition, and realistic costs.

Short Answer: Yes

A building permit is required to demolish a garage in California — even a detached one you built yourself decades ago. There are no size thresholds that exempt garage demolitions from the permit requirement, and submitting without a permit can result in fines and a stop-work order. Here is what the process actually involves.

Why a Permit Is Required

California's Building Code (Title 24, Part 2) requires a permit for all demolition of structures. The permit process exists to ensure:

  • Utilities (gas, electric, water) are properly disconnected before the structure comes down
  • An asbestos survey has been completed by a certified inspector
  • The work is done by a CSLB-licensed contractor
  • Neighboring structures and properties are not damaged
  • Debris is disposed of at a legal, licensed facility

The permit is issued by your local city's Building and Safety department — not the county, and not the state. If you live in a city (which most Southern California residents do), the city desk is your starting point.

The Garage Demolition Permit Process — Step by Step

  • Asbestos survey: A Cal/OSHA-certified inspector inspects and samples the garage for asbestos-containing materials. Required under SCAQMD Rule 1403 for any structure 100 sq ft or larger — which covers virtually all garages.
  • Utility disconnection: Contact the gas utility (SoCalGas), electric utility (SCE or city electric), and water utility to schedule formal disconnects. You will need written clearance letters for the permit application.
  • Permit application submittal: Submit to Building & Safety with the asbestos survey, site plan, and contractor license number (CSLB license required).
  • Plan check: City reviews the application. Simple detached garage demos can be approved over the counter or within 1–3 weeks. Some cities batch-review and take longer.
  • Permit issuance and demolition: Once the permit is in hand, demolition can proceed. The permit is valid for 180 days and must be posted on-site.
  • Final inspection: Some cities require a final inspection to confirm the site is properly graded and all debris is removed.

Garage Demolition for an ADU — California's Parking Rule

Good News for ADU Builders

Under California Government Code §65852.2, cities generally cannot require you to replace parking that is eliminated when a garage is demolished to build an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU). This statewide rule overrides most local parking replacement requirements and has been in effect since 2020.

If you are knocking down a garage to build an ADU or JADU (Junior ADU), the city must also process your demolition permit simultaneously with your ADU construction permit — you do not have to go through two separate queues. ADU permit applications must be approved within 60 days of a complete submission.

Do I Need to Remove the Concrete Slab?

Removing the concrete slab is not always required — it depends on what you plan to do with the space afterward. If you are building an ADU where the garage stood, you will likely want the slab removed or rebuilt to ADU specifications. If the space is becoming landscaping or lawn, some property owners choose to leave the slab in place (with city approval). Ask your demolition contractor and city planning department to confirm what is required for your specific project.

Garage Demo Costs in Southern California

All-in costs for a detached garage demolition — including permit, asbestos survey, demo labor, and debris hauling — run as follows:

  • Single-car detached garage (200–300 sq ft): $4,000 – $7,500
  • Two-car detached garage (400–600 sq ft): $7,000 – $12,000
  • Garage with thick concrete slab removal: Add $1,500 – $4,000
  • If asbestos abatement is needed: Add $1,500 – $5,000 depending on quantity found
Pre-1978 garages: Asbestos is commonly found in the drywall texture, floor tile mastic, and roofing felt of garages built before 1978. Pre-1940 garages may also have asbestos in plaster walls. Budget for potential abatement before you commit to a timeline.
