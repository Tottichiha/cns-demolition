Partial Demolition vs. Full Demolition in California — Which Do You Need?

How California permit requirements and asbestos rules apply differently depending on the scope of your project.

Not every demolition project is a full teardown. California property owners frequently need to remove a portion of a structure — one wing of a house, a rear addition, a load-bearing wall — without demolishing the entire building. Understanding how the permit process and asbestos rules apply to these partial projects prevents costly surprises.

Permit and Survey Requirements by Project Type

Common Partial Demolition Scenarios

Removing a Rear Addition or Second Story

This is one of the most structurally complex partial demolitions because the removed section is attached to the remaining structure. Shoring and temporary bracing are required to support the remaining structure during demolition. A demolition permit is required for the section being removed, and the remaining structure will typically require an inspection to confirm it is structurally sound and weather-tight before the permit is finaled. An engineer's review of the shoring plan is standard.

Full Interior Gut-Out (Keeping Shell and Foundation)

Stripping a house to its studs — removing all drywall, flooring, ceilings, insulation, and interior partitions while keeping the exterior walls, roof, and foundation — is typically permitted as an alteration/renovation rather than a demolition. However, SCAQMD Rule 1403's asbestos survey requirement applies to any renovation disturbing more than 100 square feet of material in structures 100 sq ft or larger. Pre-1978 homes being gut-rehabbed almost always trigger the survey requirement.

Removing a Specific Room or Section of a Non-Structural Wall

Selective interior demolition for remodels — opening walls, removing a single room's finishes, or creating an open floor plan by removing non-structural partitions — generally falls under a building/alteration permit. If any walls contain asbestos (common in textured drywall and plaster), abatement is required before demolition of those specific elements, even if the overall project scope is small.

Demolishing One Unit of a Multi-Unit Building

If you own a multi-unit building and want to remove one attached unit (for example, to create a larger end unit), this is a structural partial demolition requiring both a demolition permit and a building permit for the modifications to the remaining units. If the building is in a rent-controlled city and the affected unit is occupied, RSO demolition requirements also apply to the occupied unit.

Why Asbestos Applies to Partial Projects Too

This surprises many property owners. SCAQMD Rule 1403 applies to renovation as well as demolition — it is not limited to full teardowns. The threshold is disturbance of more than 100 square feet of any suspect material in a structure that is 100 square feet or larger. A gut renovation of a single bathroom in a pre-1978 home can easily exceed this threshold if the floor tile, wall texture, or ceiling material contains asbestos.

Practical takeaway: If you are renovating an older California home — even partially — budget for an asbestos survey before the contractor starts pulling materials. The survey cost ($500–$1,500) is trivial compared to the SCAQMD fine for proceeding without one ($20,000/day), and it protects your contractor from Cal/OSHA liability for exposing workers to asbestos without appropriate controls.
