How Much Does House Demolition Cost in California?

Honest pricing breakdowns, what drives costs up, and how to budget your complete project.

House demolition costs in California run higher than national averages for good reason: stricter environmental regulations, higher labor rates, expensive disposal fees at California landfills, and mandatory asbestos surveys all add to the bottom line. Here is what you can realistically expect to pay in Southern California.

What Drives the Price Higher

Asbestos or Lead Abatement

Abatement can add $2,000 to $15,000+ depending on quantity and type of material found in the survey.

Concrete Foundations

Removing a slab or basement adds $1,500 to $6,000 or more, depending on thickness and reinforcement.

Multi-Story Structures

Two-story homes require more careful sequencing and crane or high-reach equipment, increasing labor time.

Difficult Site Access

Narrow lots, shared driveways, or properties near other structures require hand-demolition in tight areas — labor-intensive and slower.

Pool Demolition

Removing or filling a pool adds $2,500 to $10,000. Partial fill (abandonment) is cheaper than full removal.

Septic System Removal

If the property has a septic tank, removal or abandonment-in-place adds $1,000 to $4,000.

What Should Be Included in a Demolition Quote?

Before signing any contract, confirm that the quote covers:

  • All labor and equipment for structure takedown
  • All debris removal and legal disposal at a licensed facility
  • Rough grading of the lot after demo
  • Permit acquisition and SCAQMD notification handling
  • What is not included (asbestos abatement, pool, foundation, etc.)

A vague quote that lists only a lump sum without scope details often comes with change orders later. Get a written scope of work, not just a number.

Can I Save Money by Hiring the Cheapest Bidder?

The lowest bid rarely means the best value in California demolition. Contractors who bid significantly below market are often cutting corners on disposal (illegal dumping, which can create liability for the property owner), skipping required permits, or not carrying adequate insurance. Always verify CSLB license status at cslb.ca.gov before signing anything.
