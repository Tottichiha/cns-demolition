How Long Does a Demolition Permit Take in California?

Realistic timelines, common bottlenecks, and how to keep your project on schedule.

The most common scheduling mistake property owners make is assuming they can pull a demo permit the same week they want the wrecking ball to show up. In California, a full residential demolition permit involves multiple agencies, mandatory notification windows, and a plan check — none of which happen overnight. Here is an honest breakdown of what to expect.

The Five Stages of a California Demo Permit

1. Pre-Application: Asbestos and Lead Survey (1–3 weeks before submitting)

Before the city will accept your demolition permit application, you need a signed asbestos survey report from a Cal/OSHA-certified inspector. This is required under SCAQMD Rule 1403 for all structures 100 square feet or larger regardless of age. Plan for one to three weeks to schedule the inspector, receive the written report, and act on any findings.

If your home was built before 1978, lead-based paint testing is also standard. Pre-1940 structures almost universally have both lead paint and asbestos in plaster, pipe insulation, or flooring.

2. Application Submittal to Building & Safety (Day 1)

Most California cities require you to submit to their Building and Safety division (not the county). Submittal packages typically include the asbestos survey, site plan, proof of ownership, and contractor license information. Your demolition contractor must hold a valid CSLB license — verify any contractor at cslb.ca.gov.

3. Plan Check (2–6 weeks)

The city's plan check team reviews the package for compliance with the California Building Code, local grading and drainage rules, and sometimes fire department access requirements. Simple structures in cities with expedited review close in two weeks. Larger structures or backlogged departments can take six weeks or more.

Pro tip: Many cities offer a paid expedited plan check that cuts review time by 30–50%. Ask about this at the public counter — it is often worth the fee if your construction loan is running interest.

4. Utility Disconnection Clearances (Concurrent — 1–4 weeks)

The city will not issue the demolition permit until all utilities are formally disconnected and the utility company has issued a clearance letter. This means:

  • Natural gas: SoCalGas service termination and cap, with written confirmation
  • Electricity: SCE or municipal electric utility disconnect and meter pull
  • Water & sewer: City utility department cap-off at the main

Call utilities the moment you submit your permit application so the clearances are ready when the city completes plan check.

5. SCAQMD Notification Window (10 business days minimum)

If regulated asbestos-containing materials were found and abatement is required, California law mandates a minimum 10 business day notification to SCAQMD before abatement begins. This window can run concurrently with city plan check if timed correctly, but it cannot be skipped.

What Slows Everything Down

  • Incomplete submittal — missing survey report, unsigned documents, or unlicensed contractor listed
  • Utility companies scheduling disconnect crews weeks out (call early)
  • Properties in historical overlay zones (additional review required)
  • Rent-controlled rental units — the Los Angeles Housing Department (LAHD), or equivalent board in Santa Monica, West Hollywood, etc., must issue a written clearance before Building & Safety will proceed
  • Slope, grading, or drainage conditions requiring a geology or soils report
