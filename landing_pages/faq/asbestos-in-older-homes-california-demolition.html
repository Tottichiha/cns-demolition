Asbestos in Older Homes — What to Expect Before Demolition in California

Where asbestos hides by building era, what it costs to remove, and how it fits into your demolition timeline.

Asbestos is the most common complication in residential demolition projects in California. It is not a sign that something went wrong with your property — it was a legal, widely used building material for most of the 20th century. Knowing where it tends to hide based on your home's construction era lets you budget accurately and avoid timeline surprises.

Asbestos Risk by Building Era

Pre-1940 Homes — Near-Universal Risk

Homes built before 1940 almost universally contain asbestos-containing materials in multiple systems. Expect to find:

  • Textured plaster on walls and ceilings (the plaster itself often contains asbestos)
  • Pipe insulation wrapping (often looks like white or grey wrap around heating pipes)
  • Floor tile and mastic under later flooring layers
  • Roofing felt and certain flat roofing materials
  • Boiler and furnace insulation
Budget expectation: Full abatement in pre-1940 homes commonly runs $8,000 to $20,000+ before demolition can proceed.
Pre-1978 Homes — Significant Risk

The period from 1945 to 1978 was peak asbestos use in residential construction. Common findings include:

  • Acoustic spray (popcorn) ceiling texture — the most commonly found ACM in this era
  • 9×9 inch vinyl floor tiles (the size is a near-certain indicator) and their black mastic adhesive
  • Heating duct tape and duct wrap insulation
  • Certain ceiling and floor tiles in kitchens and bathrooms
  • Siding shingles (some brands contained asbestos)
Budget expectation: Abatement in this era typically runs $2,500 to $10,000 depending on which systems are affected.
Post-1978 Homes — Lower But Not Zero Risk

While asbestos was phased out of most residential products by the early 1980s, some products with asbestos content remained in use into the 1980s and even 1990s. A survey is still required by law. Occasional findings include:

  • Certain floor tile and mastic products used into the early 1980s
  • Roofing materials and felts from some manufacturers
  • Textured ceiling compound (joint compound) — some formulations in the early 1980s
Budget expectation: Abatement in post-1978 homes, if needed at all, is usually limited and runs $1,000 to $4,000.

The Abatement Process — What Actually Happens

🔍

Survey

Certified inspector samples all suspect materials, lab confirms asbestos content.

📋

SCAQMD Notice

10 business day mandatory notification window before abatement can begin.

🛡️

Containment

Licensed abatement crew seals the work area, establishes negative air pressure.

🚛

Removal & Disposal

ACMs wet-stripped, bagged in labeled containers, transported to a licensed hazmat facility.

✅

Clearance Test

Air sampling confirms fiber levels are below regulatory limits before demolition proceeds.

Does Finding Asbestos Delay My Demo Significantly?

It depends on the quantity. The mandatory 10-business-day SCAQMD notification window before abatement begins is fixed — it cannot be expedited. However, if your contractor submits the SCAQMD notification while the city is still doing plan check (which is a common practice), the two can run concurrently, minimizing the net delay. Abatement work itself on a single-family home typically takes two to five days once crews mobilize.

The projects where asbestos causes the most delay are those where the property owner did not budget for it or where the survey was not conducted early enough. Starting the survey process at the same time you engage a contractor — not after — keeps your timeline intact.

Can Asbestos Be Left in Place?

No — not for full demolition. "In-place management" (encapsulation or enclosure) is a legitimate approach for asbestos in occupied buildings that are being renovated, not demolished. When a structure is being torn down, all regulated asbestos-containing materials must be abated before demolition begins. There is no legal pathway for demolishing around intact ACMs in California.
