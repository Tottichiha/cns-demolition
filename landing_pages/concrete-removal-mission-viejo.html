Concrete Removal in a 1970s South OC Neighborhood

Mission Viejo's original housing stock comes with original concrete — driveways poured in 1969, patios laid in 1975, pool decks finished in 1977. Fifty years of South Orange County sun, occasional freeze cycles, and soil movement from the clay-heavy hillside soil that underlies much of Mission Viejo means that concrete is cracked, heaved, and past its useful life on a lot of these properties.

Removal in these neighborhoods is more physical than technical. The concrete is typically 4-inch unreinforced or lightly reinforced — easier to break than 1940s commercial concrete — but the access situation varies. Many Mission Viejo homes have side yard gates and fencing that limit where equipment can go. Some of the hillside properties in Aegean Hills and Casta del Sol have grade changes that affect how broken concrete gets staged and loaded. C&S assesses access at the site visit and confirms equipment positioning before scheduling.

For pool deck removal specifically — which Mission Viejo generates in volume as aging pools are filled in or replastered — the coordination with the pool contractor is critical. C&S removes the deck, and the pool contractor handles the fill or replaster work. The sequencing matters: concrete deck out first, pool work second, so the equipment staging for deck removal doesn't interfere with the pool work zone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does concrete removal in Mission Viejo require a permit?

Removing a concrete driveway, patio, or walkway without replacement typically doesn't require a permit in Mission Viejo. Removing a foundation as part of a permitted demolition project is covered under the demolition permit. Retaining wall removal may require a grading permit depending on the height and the slope condition behind it. C&S confirms the permit requirement for each specific scope with the City of Mission Viejo Building & Safety Division before scheduling.

Is the clay soil in Mission Viejo a problem for concrete removal?

Clay-heavy soil expands and contracts with moisture — which is why so many Mission Viejo driveways and patios have heaved and cracked. The soil condition itself doesn't complicate concrete removal much, but it's worth knowing before you pour new concrete. Many property owners use concrete removal as an opportunity to assess subbase conditions and have the soil engineer evaluate whether additional base preparation is needed before the new pour. C&S removes the old concrete and can coordinate with your civil engineer on subbase conditions once the concrete is out.

Can C&S remove concrete in a backyard with a narrow gate access?

Yes — narrow side yard and gate access is common in Mission Viejo's 1970s and 1980s tract layouts. C&S uses smaller equipment for backyard access where full-size machinery can't enter. Concrete is broken by hand jack or small compactor, staged in manageable loads, and carried through the gate access to the haul truck. Access dimensions are confirmed at the site walk so equipment is sized appropriately on delivery day.

What happens to the concrete after removal?

All concrete is hauled off-site to a licensed concrete recycling or disposal facility. Concrete is one of the most recyclable construction materials — it's crushed and used as base material for road construction and new concrete pours. C&S doesn't leave broken concrete on the property or suggest burying it on-site. Everything goes.
