Why Irvine Garage Demo Projects Get Delayed

Irvine was designed — every lot, every utility run, every setback — which means detached garages in Irvine's planned villages sit on precisely located pads with known structural specs and documented utility connections. The demolition itself is predictable. What's less predictable is the approval process.

Most Irvine garage demolition projects are tied to ADU conversions, backyard expansions, or teardown-and-rebuild of an existing structure. In every case, the project has to clear the village HOA's Architectural Review Committee before the City of Irvine Building Division will issue a demolition permit. That sequence is non-negotiable — and ARC meetings happen monthly, not weekly.

Beyond HOA approval, garage demolition in Irvine requires utility disconnection coordination with Irvine Ranch Water District, SoCalGas, and SCE before the permit issues. For attached garages being removed as part of a larger project, the structural interface between the garage and the main house has to be clearly defined in the demolition plan to satisfy the plan check review.

Common Garage Demo Scenarios in Irvine

  • Detached Garage Removed for ADU Construction

    State ADU law has created demand for backyard ADU pads throughout Irvine. When a detached garage occupies the space where an ADU is planned, demolition is step one. Irvine Building Division's ADU permitting process and the demolition permit can often be coordinated so the ADU construction permit is staged behind the demo completion. C&S coordinates the sequencing with your architect and the Building Division plan check staff.

  • Attached Garage Removal for House Addition

    Attached garage demolition in Irvine requires careful definition of the structural separation between the garage and the residence. The shared wall, roof tie, and foundation connection each affect how the demolition is sequenced and what temporary shoring is required. C&S reviews the structural drawings with your architect before demo begins — we don't guess on structural interfaces.

  • Detached Garage Teardown and Rebuild

    When an existing detached garage is being replaced with a new one — typically to add square footage, update the structure, or accommodate a new layout — demolition permit and new construction permit are typically handled as a combined project. C&S coordinates the demo timing with your GC so the new construction permit is issued before demo begins, and the site is ready for the new structure immediately after clearance.

  • Garage Slab Removal

    When the garage structure is removed but the concrete slab remains, new construction often requires slab removal before the foundation is poured. C&S removes garage slabs as a standalone scope or as part of the full garage demolition package. Concrete from demolition is hauled off-site — not left behind as fill material.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my Irvine HOA have to approve a garage demolition?

In virtually all of Irvine's planned communities, yes. The HOA Architectural Review Committee has authority over demolition and construction projects on properties within the community. The specific documentation required varies by village — some ARCs want a full demo plan; others want a site plan and project description letter. C&S helps prepare the submission package for your specific village. The HOA approval is a precondition for the City demolition permit in most cases.

Is asbestos testing required for an Irvine garage built in 1985?

Yes. SCAQMD Rule 1403 requires a certified asbestos and lead-containing materials survey for all structures before demolition permits are issued — regardless of when the structure was built. A garage built in 1985 or 2000 is subject to the same survey requirement as one built in 1968. There is no construction-date exemption under the rule. The City of Irvine will not issue a demolition permit without the completed survey on file.

Can C&S remove the concrete slab in addition to the garage structure?

Yes. Slab removal is included in the scope when it's required for new construction. C&S removes the concrete, hauls it off-site to a licensed disposal facility, and confirms the site is graded to the condition your contractor needs for the next phase. Slab removal scope and depth are confirmed before work begins.

How much notice do I need to give my HOA before demolition work starts?

That depends on your village CC&Rs. Most Irvine HOAs require ARC approval before any permitted work begins — and some require a separate notice to the HOA property manager with the permit number and scheduled start date. C&S reviews the relevant CC&R provisions with you and follows whatever notice requirements your village mandates. Surprises to the HOA property manager on demo day create unnecessary friction.
