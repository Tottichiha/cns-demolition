Why Santa Monica Concrete Fails the Way It Does

Santa Monica sits on a marine terrace underlain by expansive clay soil — the kind that swells with moisture and shrinks in dry periods. This repeated expansion-and-contraction cycle stresses concrete slabs in ways that inland concrete doesn't experience. A driveway or patio poured without adequate base preparation or expansion joints will start showing cracks and heaving within 15–20 years in Santa Monica. Many of the driveways being removed today were poured in the 1970s and have simply run their service life.

The salt-air exposure accelerates corrosion of any embedded rebar, particularly within the first few blocks of the beach. Ocean Park and the beach-adjacent blocks of Sunset Park see the fastest salt-air concrete degradation — by the time a property is ready for redevelopment, the original concrete often has spalling and surface delamination that makes removal more work than a clean inland slab. C&S assesses the concrete condition at the site visit and confirms whether it changes the removal approach or equipment needed.

Before you pour new concrete in Santa Monica: If your old slab heaved or cracked prematurely, ask your civil engineer to evaluate the subbase before the new concrete is poured. Expansive clay soil requires proper subbase preparation — crushed aggregate, vapor barrier, and sometimes soil treatment — to give the new pour a fighting chance at a full service life. C&S removes the old concrete; the subbase prep is your concrete contractor's and civil engineer's call.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does concrete removal require a permit in Santa Monica?

Removing a private driveway or patio slab without alteration to the grade typically doesn't require a permit. Removing a foundation as part of a permitted demolition is covered under the demolition permit. Retaining wall removal over a certain height threshold requires a grading permit. The City of Santa Monica Building & Safety Division confirms permit requirements for specific scopes. C&S checks the permit status for each project rather than assuming.

Is the clay soil in Santa Monica a problem for concrete removal equipment?

Not for the removal itself — clay soil doesn't meaningfully affect how concrete is broken and loaded. Where clay soil matters is what happens after the concrete is out. Expansive clay exposed by concrete removal can shift equipment slightly if the surface is wet — C&S positions equipment on stable surfaces and avoids working in saturated subgrade conditions where possible. The primary issue is what happens to the subbase before new concrete is poured — that's the contractor's and civil engineer's domain.

Can C&S haul concrete to a recycling facility instead of a landfill?

Yes — C&S hauls broken concrete to licensed concrete recycling facilities. Concrete is one of the most recyclable construction materials and is accepted at multiple recycling yards in the LA area. Diverting concrete from landfill is standard practice, not an add-on service. All C&S concrete disposal goes to licensed facilities — recycling where available, licensed disposal otherwise.
