Three Types of Malibu Demolition Projects

Malibu has the most complex demolition environment in the Los Angeles area. The combination of hillside terrain, California Coastal Zone regulations, LA County jurisdiction (not City of Malibu), private septic systems, and fire-prone brush means no two projects here look the same. Before planning any scope, it's important to identify which of the three project types applies — because each has a different permit path and a different set of pre-demo requirements.

Post-Fire Teardown

Fire debris contains concentrated hazardous materials beyond what standard demolition waste involves — charred asbestos, combustion byproducts, melted compounds. Specialized testing, disposal documentation, and Cal/OSHA compliance apply. LA County may activate expedited permit pathways following declared disasters. Insurance coordination is typically required.

Standard Estate Demolition

Planned teardown-and-rebuild of a standing residential structure. LA County Building & Safety permit required. Asbestos and lead survey required for all structures per SCAQMD Rule 1403. If the property is within the Coastal Zone, a Coastal Development Permit determination must be made before submitting the building permit application.

Emergency / Red-Tag Demo

Structures condemned by LA County Building & Safety following a slide, fire damage, or structural failure. Emergency demolition orders require rapid response while still meeting permit requirements. C&S can mobilize quickly on red-tagged properties — call directly for emergency situations.

Permit Requirements for Malibu Demolitions

Two separate agencies may have jurisdiction over your Malibu demolition project.

LA County Building & Safety (LACBS) — Always Required

Malibu contracts its building and safety services to Los Angeles County. All demolition permits for Malibu properties are issued by LACBS, not by the City of Malibu. The application requires the asbestos/lead survey report, utility disconnection documentation, and site plan. C&S manages this process directly.

California Coastal Commission CDP — Situational

Because Malibu falls almost entirely within the California Coastal Zone, a Coastal Development Permit may be required depending on your parcel's exact location. Properties within approximately 300 feet of mean high tide, or within 100 feet of a wetland or ESHA, typically require a CDP. Some demolitions qualify for categorical exemptions. C&S works alongside your land use attorney or permit consultant to determine applicability — we don't make that determination ourselves, and neither should any demolition contractor.

SCAQMD Rule 1403 — Asbestos & Lead Survey

Rule 1403 of the South Coast Air Quality Management District requires an asbestos and lead-containing materials survey for every structure before demolition permits are issued. This is not limited to older buildings — all structures require the survey regardless of construction date. If regulated asbestos is found, a 10-business-day notification to SCAQMD must be filed and abatement completed before demolition begins. C&S coordinates with licensed environmental consultants for survey and abatement scope.

Septic System Decommissioning

Most Malibu properties are on private septic systems, not municipal sewer. Decommissioning the septic system before demolition is a separate scope performed by a licensed septic contractor. C&S does not perform septic decommissioning but coordinates with the septic contractor to sequence the work correctly — septic decommissioning must happen before demolition begins.

Utility Disconnections

LADWP or Southern California Edison for electrical, SoCalGas for gas, and any private well or water service must all be formally terminated before demolition begins. C&S coordinates disconnection scheduling and assembles the written confirmations required by LACBS.

Malibu's Terrain — What Different Areas Actually Require

Malibu stretches 21 miles along PCH, and the terrain and access conditions change significantly depending on where the property sits.

  • PCH-adjacent and beachfront lots: Flat or low-slope lots with coastal access. Limited staging space. Street work on PCH requires coordination with Caltrans and careful traffic management. Many of these properties are within the most restrictive coastal zone classifications for CDP purposes.
  • Point Dume and Cliffside Road area: Elevated coastal bluff properties with stunning views and specific bluff stability considerations. Equipment must be sized to protect bluff edges. Some properties have limited road access from PCH requiring careful routing.
  • Malibu Park and Malibu Knolls: Inland hillside neighborhoods above PCH. Steeper grades, narrower canyon roads, and private driveways. Access planning is required before mobilization. Some lots in these areas qualify for CDP exemptions due to their inland position within the Coastal Zone.
  • Civic Center and Las Virgenes area: Transitional area between coastal Malibu and the Santa Monica Mountains. Some properties have easier access but are still subject to Coastal Zone regulations. Growing teardown-and-rebuild activity in this corridor.
  • Broad Beach and Trancas Canyon: Upper Malibu, higher concentration of large estate properties. Gated entrances on some roads require advance coordination. Larger structure footprints mean longer demo timelines and more debris hauls.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who issues demolition permits in Malibu?

LA County Building & Safety issues all demolition permits in Malibu. The City of Malibu contracts building and safety services to the County. For Coastal Zone properties, a Coastal Development Permit from the California Coastal Commission may also be required — the determination depends on the specific parcel location and scope of work.

Does every Malibu demolition need a Coastal Development Permit?

Not automatically. CDP requirement depends on where your parcel sits within the Coastal Zone. Properties within approximately 300 feet of the high tide line or 100 feet of a wetland typically require a CDP. Some demolitions qualify for categorical exemptions. C&S works alongside your land use consultant or attorney to navigate this determination — we don't skip this step.

What does SCAQMD Rule 1403 require for Malibu demolitions?

Rule 1403 requires an asbestos and lead-containing materials survey for every structure before demolition permits can be issued — this applies to all structures regardless of age or construction date. If regulated asbestos is found, SCAQMD must be notified at least 10 business days before demo begins, and abatement must be completed first. C&S coordinates with licensed environmental consultants for both survey and abatement if needed.

What about the septic system on my Malibu property?

Septic decommissioning is a separate scope from demolition and must be completed before demo begins. C&S does not perform septic work — we coordinate with a licensed septic contractor and sequence both scopes so there's no delay. During the site visit, C&S identifies septic system location and flags it for the septic contractor.
