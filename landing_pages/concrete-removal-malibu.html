Terrain Shapes the Method — Malibu Site Types

PCH Coastal Flat

Good equipment access, room to stage debris, standard truck turnaround. Main considerations: neighbor proximity, ocean-side drop-offs, and whether any work is within the Coastal Zone encroachment boundary. Concrete removal from flat coastal lots is typically the most straightforward Malibu job.

Bluff-Top Properties

Equipment operates close to the bluff edge in some cases. C&S establishes setback limits for any heavy equipment near the bluff line. Debris staging is kept well back from the edge. On bluff-top pool deck removals, equipment access from the house side only — no equipment beyond the structure footprint.

Canyon Road Properties

Single-lane driveways with sharp turns limit truck access. Concrete is broken into pieces small enough to hand-carry or walk-behind-stage before any truck loading. On narrow canyon roads, debris removal is scheduled in windows that don't block through traffic — critical on PCH feeder roads.

Steep Hillside Parcels

Steep grades require concrete to be broken at elevation and staged down to a truck access point. No dragging slabs down slopes — everything goes through controlled piece-breaking and manual staging. Equipment is sized to fit the access available, not to what would be fastest on a flat lot.

Method Selection

Hydraulic Breaker (Excavator-Mounted)

Fastest method for large flat areas with excavator access. Breaks concrete quickly into bucket-loadable pieces. Used on accessible driveways, large patio slabs, and pool decks where machine access covers the full removal zone.

Concrete Saw Cutting

Used for clean boundary cuts on partial removal — preventing cracking in the remaining slab — and for scoring thick concrete before hydraulic breaking. Flat-saw and hand-saw methods depending on geometry and access constraints.

Hand Equipment (Jackhammers & Walk-Behind Breakers)

Required where excavator access is impossible: narrow canyon driveways, steep hillside terraces, tight pool surrounds, and under-deck areas. Slower, but the only viable approach in areas where machine swing radius doesn't fit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit for concrete removal in Malibu?

Encroachment permits from LA County Public Works are required for concrete work that involves the public right-of-way — driveway apron near the street, work near PCH access points. Interior site concrete (pool decks, patios, slabs) generally does not require a standalone permit unless it's part of a larger permitted project or involves grading in the Coastal Zone. C&S advises during the site visit.

How does rebar affect the removal job?

Rebar-reinforced concrete takes longer to break and requires cutting the steel after sections are separated. Rebar is removed from the concrete pieces, bundled, and recycled as scrap metal separately from the concrete aggregate. Commercial-grade driveways and structural foundation work are typically the most heavily reinforced scopes in Malibu.

Can partial driveway removal be done with clean edges?

Yes. Saw-cutting creates a straight, clean joint at the removal boundary so new concrete poured adjacent to the removal area doesn't crack prematurely. Partial driveway replacement is common when only a damaged section needs to come out — C&S cuts to a clean boundary and removes the designated section cleanly.

Do hauling distances to licensed facilities affect the scope?

Yes. Malibu's location adds haul time compared to urban LA concrete removal jobs. Licensed recycling facilities are not on PCH — they're in the inland areas of LA County. This is reflected in the site visit assessment when C&S puts together the lump-sum quote. There are no surprise haul charges added after the job.
