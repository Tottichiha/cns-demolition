What Interior Demo Actually Looks Like in a Pre-War Santa Monica Home

Santa Monica's residential neighborhoods contain a significant amount of pre-World War II housing stock — particularly in Ocean Park, portions of Sunset Park, and the older blocks between Lincoln Boulevard and the beach. These homes were built in the 1920s and 1930s when construction practices and material composition were entirely different from anything built after 1978.

Lead paint in pre-1940 residential construction isn't a maybe — it was the standard. Every painted surface in a 1932 Santa Monica bungalow was painted with lead-based paint. Not some surfaces. Every surface. Selective interior demolition in these homes requires an RRP (Renovation, Repair, and Painting) approach with proper containment and debris handling, regardless of whether an asbestos survey has been triggered. Lead dust from disturbed paint in these homes is a genuine exposure risk that C&S addresses before work starts — not after.

Lead in pre-1940 Santa Monica homes: C&S treats lead paint as present-until-proven-absent in any Santa Monica home built before 1940. This means dust containment, negative air pressure in work zones where practical, and debris disposal as lead-containing waste where the paint condition warrants it. We don't approach a 1928 Ocean Park bungalow the same way we'd approach a 1995 Irvine production home — the material reality is different and the demolition approach has to match.

Asbestos in these older homes adds the second layer. Original plaster in pre-1945 California homes sometimes contains asbestos. Floor tile adhesive, if original, dates to the era when asbestos-containing adhesive was standard. Pipe insulation on original steam or hot water systems almost certainly has regulated asbestos content. The SCAQMD survey identifies what's present — but the approach to interior demo in these homes starts with the assumption that multiple categories of regulated material may be present.

Post-1980 Santa Monica homes — in Sunset Park's newer blocks, in mid-city condominiums, in newer construction along Ocean Avenue — present a different risk profile and move faster through the pre-demo assessment process.

Interior Demo Scopes in Santa Monica

Kitchen Gut

Full kitchen demolition in homes where the original galley layout is being opened up or the kitchen is fully rebuilt. Lead paint on cabinet interiors, original linoleum flooring with asbestos-containing adhesive, and original plumbing rough-in — each assessed before any demo begins. C&S returns a clean shell with utility stub-outs accessible for the new kitchen installation.

Bathroom Tearout

Original tile-on-mortar bathrooms in 1920s–1930s Santa Monica homes are heavy material. Mortar bed alone can weigh hundreds of pounds in a small bathroom. C&S accounts for this in the debris plan — debris volume from a mortar-bed bathroom is 3–4x a standard drywall-substrate bathroom. Quoted accordingly.

Plaster Wall Removal

Original lath-and-plaster walls in pre-war Santa Monica homes may contain asbestos in the plaster mix itself. C&S has the plaster sampled before removal — standard drywall demo practices do not apply to pre-war plaster in California homes. If asbestos is confirmed, abatement precedes C&S's structural demo work.

Full-Gut Interior Demo

Complete interior demolition to studs and subfloor for major whole-home remodels. Pre-war homes typically require abatement of multiple material categories before full-gut demo can begin. C&S coordinates the abatement sequencing so that each material category is handled in the correct regulatory order before structural demo proceeds.

Open-Plan Wall Removal

Load-bearing wall removal in Santa Monica's bungalow stock — required for the open kitchen/living conversions that are standard in Westside remodels. Structural engineer's beam and shoring plan required before any structural wall comes down. C&S works from the engineer's drawings exclusively on structural scope.

ADU Interior Conversion

Interior demolition to prep a garage, basement, or ancillary structure for ADU conversion. If the space being converted has pre-1979 construction, the same asbestos and lead considerations apply. C&S scopes the interior demo to match the ADU permit drawings — only what the plans specify comes out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does interior demo in Santa Monica require a permit?

Cosmetic interior demo — removing non-structural drywall, tile, cabinets, and flooring being replaced in kind — generally doesn't require a standalone demolition permit in Santa Monica. Structural work (wall removal affecting load-bearing elements, changes to MEP systems) requires permits under the remodel or structural permit. The City of Santa Monica Building & Safety Division makes the permit determination based on the specific scope. C&S confirms the requirement before scheduling.

How much does lead paint abatement add to interior demo cost in an older Santa Monica home?

Abatement cost depends on the extent of lead-containing paint being disturbed, the material condition of the paint, and whether full abatement or containment-and-disturbance protocol is being used. For selective interior demo (a kitchen or bathroom), the incremental abatement cost is manageable. For a full-gut interior, abatement of extensive lead paint can be a significant project cost. C&S identifies the scope at the site walk and provides a realistic estimate of the abatement component before you commit to the project timeline.

Can interior demo happen in an occupied Santa Monica home?

Yes, but in pre-war homes with confirmed or probable lead paint, occupied-home demo requires more extensive containment than in newer construction. Dust containment barriers, negative air pressure in the work zone, and daily cleanup protocols protect the occupied portions of the home. C&S confirms the containment plan with you before work starts in any occupied pre-war Santa Monica home.
