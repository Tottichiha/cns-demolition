Santa Monica Demolition Isn't Just a Building Department Problem

Santa Monica has been a rent-controlled city since 1979. The Santa Monica Rent Control Board has jurisdiction over the demolition of any residential units that fall under rent control — which includes most multi-family residential buildings and many single-family homes that were previously used as rentals. If your property has or had rent-controlled tenants, the Rent Control Board's demolition permit process runs parallel to (and often longer than) the City Building & Safety permit process.

The Rent Control Board requires tenant relocation assistance payments, documented proper notice, and a hearing if any tenant contests the demolition. This is not a formality — contested demolition hearings in Santa Monica have delayed projects by a year or more. Property owners who don't engage an attorney experienced with Santa Monica rent control before initiating demolition often discover this the hard way, mid-process, after the building permit is already approved.

C&S handles the demolition work. You need an attorney for the Rent Control Board.

Before you call a demolition contractor about a Santa Monica rental property, call a land use attorney who knows Santa Monica rent control. The Rent Control Board process must be completed before demolition can begin — and that process takes longer than most property owners expect. C&S can provide a demolition quote and timeline estimate for the physical work, but we cannot advise on rent control compliance. Those are separate processes and they must both be done right.

For owner-occupied single-family homes with no rent control tenants, the process is simpler — City Building & Safety permit, SCAQMD survey, utility disconnections, and demolition. Santa Monica's building permit process is thorough and typically takes 4–6 weeks for a complete demolition permit application.

Santa Monica Neighborhoods — What Makes Each One Different for Demo

Ocean Park

Southern Santa Monica, dense bungalow and small apartment stock from the 1920s–1950s. Very high probability of asbestos in multiple material categories and lead paint throughout. Rent control dense — most multi-family here is covered. Pre-demo attorney consultation is essential before any Ocean Park rental property demolition.

North of Montana

Affluent single-family neighborhood. Older homes from 1920s–1960s mixed with newer construction. Single-family owner-occupied homes here bypass the Rent Control Board process. Still require SCAQMD survey — pre-1979 homes have real asbestos risk. Larger lots provide better equipment staging than other Santa Monica neighborhoods.

Sunset Park

South of Pico, mostly single-family with some duplexes. Mix of 1940s–1970s construction. Some properties covered by rent control, some not. Pre-demo ownership history matters — verify rent control status with the Santa Monica Rent Control Board before any planning begins.

Mid-City / Downtown Adjacent

Higher density residential near downtown Santa Monica and the Expo Line stations. Older apartment buildings and mixed-use structures. Heavy rent control coverage. Complex permit process. C&S can handle the physical demolition once all regulatory clearances are in place — the timeline on these projects is driven by the Rent Control Board, not the building permit.

The Permit Process — Owner-Occupied Single Family (No Rent Control Tenants)

  1. SCAQMD Rule 1403 Asbestos & Lead SurveyRequired for all structures before City Building & Safety will issue a demolition permit. Pre-1940s Santa Monica homes often yield findings in multiple material categories — lead paint, asbestos in plaster, floor tile, pipe insulation, and roofing. Budget for abatement as a realistic probability, not a contingency.
  2. Abatement (If Survey Positive)Licensed abatement contractor removes regulated materials. SCAQMD notified minimum 10 business days before abatement begins. C&S demolition follows abatement completion with clearance documentation in hand.
  3. Utility DisconnectionsSoCalGas, SCE, and Santa Monica Water Division (City utility — not LADWP). All three disconnection letters required before Building & Safety issues the demo permit. Santa Monica Water Division processes disconnect requests through the City's Public Works Department.
  4. City of Santa Monica Building & Safety Demolition PermitApplication with survey, utility letters, and site plan. Plan check review typically 4–6 weeks for complete applications. C&S prepares the complete application package.
  5. Demolition and Site ClearanceStructure demolished, debris hauled, site graded. Physical demo of a typical Santa Monica single-family home takes 4–7 days depending on size and access. Complete permit closure documentation provided.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my Santa Monica property is subject to rent control?

The Santa Monica Rent Control Board maintains a property database. Properties built before April 10, 1979 and with two or more rental units are generally covered. Some single-family rentals are also covered under specific circumstances. The Rent Control Board's website has a property lookup tool, or you can call their office directly. Do this before engaging any demolition contractor — the answer changes everything about your project timeline and budget.

Does Santa Monica have any restrictions on what can be built after demolition?

Yes — and they're significant. Santa Monica's zoning code, in combination with rent control law, has provisions that affect what can replace demolished rental housing. In some cases, replacement housing requirements apply. This is a land use planning and legal question — your attorney and architect need to address what can be built on the site before demolition proceeds. C&S provides the demolition; the redevelopment plan is your architect and attorney's domain.

My Santa Monica home was built in 1928. What should I expect from the environmental survey?

A 1928 home in Santa Monica will almost certainly have regulated materials in multiple categories. Lead paint is near-universal in pre-1940 residential construction. Asbestos-containing plaster, floor tile adhesive, pipe insulation, and roofing materials are all common findings. SCAQMD surveys of 1920s–1930s Santa Monica homes regularly require abatement of several material categories. The cost and timeline of abatement on a pre-1940 home is meaningfully different from a 1975 home — plan accordingly.
