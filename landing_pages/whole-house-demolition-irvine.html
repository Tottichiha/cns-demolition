Two Approval Processes Before Demo Starts — And One Has to Go First

Most people planning a whole house demolition in Irvine think about permits. They should be thinking about their HOA first. In virtually every master-planned village in Irvine — Woodbridge, Turtle Rock, Northwood, Westpark, Quail Hill, and the newer Great Park neighborhoods — the Architectural Review Committee has to approve any demolition project before the City of Irvine Building Division will issue a permit. Not in parallel. Before.

ARC committees in Irvine villages typically meet once a month. If you submit your application a week after the meeting cut-off, you wait another month. That adds 4 to 6 weeks to your timeline before a single permit application is even filed. Property owners who don't know this end up waiting on a schedule they didn't account for.

C&S walks through the HOA and permit process at the very beginning of every Irvine project. We identify the specific ARC requirements for your village, help prepare the documentation the committee expects, and sequence the permit application to follow the ARC approval rather than run into it.

Irvine demolition permits are issued by the City of Irvine Building Division — not Orange County Building & Safety. Irvine is an incorporated city with its own permitting authority, and the requirements differ from unincorporated OC.

The demolition itself — once permits are in hand — is usually straightforward. Irvine's flat terrain, planned underground utilities, and well-documented structure specs make the physical demo more predictable than hillside properties in other parts of Orange County. The regulatory path is where Irvine projects need active management.

Irvine Villages We Work In

Woodbridge

Turtle Rock

Hillside community with larger lots than most Irvine villages. 1970s–1980s custom and production homes. Some properties have slopes requiring excavation equipment positioning attention. Turtle Rock Community Association ARC review required.

Northwood

1980s–1990s production homes in a tight grid layout. Closer lot lines than older Irvine villages. Demolition debris management and equipment access in narrow side yards requires planning. Northwood HOA ARC process is typically faster than older village associations.

Quail Hill

Newer Irvine village — late 1990s and early 2000s construction. SCAQMD Rule 1403 still requires a certified survey before demolition regardless of construction date. HOA is active and visible. Quail Hill is adjacent to open space preserve — dust control and debris containment are taken seriously.

Portola Springs / Great Park

Irvine's newest neighborhoods. 2005 through present construction. Newer structures, newer infrastructure, newer HOA boards still establishing precedents for construction and demolition projects. HOA approval required; process may take longer than more established associations.

University Park & El Camino Real

Early Irvine villages from the late 1960s and 1970s. Oldest residential construction in the city. SCAQMD Rule 1403 survey required for all structures. Original-era construction materials often untouched by renovation — survey findings here frequently require remediation before demo can proceed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does whole house demolition take in Irvine from start to finish?

The physical demolition of a typical Irvine home takes 3–5 days. Total timeline from first engagement to cleared site is another story — HOA ARC approval (2–8 weeks depending on meeting cycle), permit processing (2–3 weeks), utility disconnection coordination (1–3 weeks), and any asbestos remediation if required. Realistic minimum from project initiation to demo start is 6–10 weeks for most Irvine villages. Starting early is the only way to control the schedule.

Does the foundation get removed during demolition?

That depends on what you're doing next. If new construction is planned on the same footprint with the same foundation, your architect and structural engineer may specify keeping the existing slab or portions of it. If the foundation is being replaced or the footprint is changing, C&S removes it as part of the scope. The decision is confirmed before work starts — not discovered mid-job.

Do Irvine homes built after 1980 still need an environmental survey?

Yes. SCAQMD Rule 1403 requires a certified asbestos and lead-containing materials survey for all structures before demolition permits are issued — regardless of construction date. There is no exemption for newer buildings. A home built in 1995 or 2005 still requires the survey before the City of Irvine will issue a demolition permit. This is a common misconception that delays Irvine projects when owners find out late in the process.

What happens if the HOA denies the demolition application?

It's uncommon for ARC committees to outright deny demolition, but they do require specific documentation formats, may request modifications to site protection plans, or ask for neighbor notification letters. Incomplete applications get tabled to the next meeting cycle. C&S helps prepare documentation that matches what each village's ARC typically expects — reducing the chances of a first-submission rejection that costs you a full meeting cycle.
