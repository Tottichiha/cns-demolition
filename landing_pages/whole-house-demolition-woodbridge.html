The Woodbridge Timeline Problem

Woodbridge was the first large planned village in Irvine to reach full build-out. Most of the single-family residences along streets like Barranca Pkwy, Bryan Ave, and the cul-de-sacs around North Lake and South Lake were completed between 1975 and 1985 — before the EPA's 1989 partial asbestos ban and well before California's own stricter standards. That means floor tile adhesive, popcorn ceilings, pipe insulation, duct tape on HVAC, and roofing felt from that era were routinely manufactured with asbestos fibers.

Under current SCAQMD Rule 1403, there is no age exemption. Every residential structure — regardless of whether it was built in 1975 or 2005 — requires a certified AHERA inspector survey before demolition permits can be issued. The distinction matters for Woodbridge specifically: surveys in neighborhoods of this vintage consistently find more positive samples than surveys in newer Irvine villages like Northwood or Stonegate.

C&S Demolition coordinates the survey, abatement contractor handoff, SCAQMD notification, and final clearance as a bundled process — so you are not managing three separate contractors before a shovel touches ground.

SCAQMD Rule 1403 — No Age Exemption

Rule 1403 applies to all structures regardless of construction date. A pre-demolition asbestos survey by a certified inspector is mandatory before any permit can be issued by the City of Irvine Building & Safety Division.

Woodbridge's 1975–1985 construction window places it in the highest-probability tier for asbestos-containing materials in floor adhesive, ceiling texture, pipe wrap, and roofing components.

The ARC Step People Miss

Woodbridge Village Association operates an Architectural Review Committee. Unlike some Irvine villages where HOA review is a courtesy process, the Woodbridge ARC has formal jurisdiction over exterior alterations — and a full demolition absolutely qualifies. You will need ARC approval before the City of Irvine Building & Safety will accept a demolition permit application.

This means the permitting sequence for a Woodbridge whole-house teardown runs: ARC application and approval → SCAQMD survey and (if necessary) abatement → City of Irvine demolition permit → utility disconnections → demolition. Skipping or reordering those steps causes permit rejections that set projects back by weeks.

We have run this sequence in Woodbridge enough times to know what the ARC wants to see in a submittal package: site plan, proposed new footprint or lot-clearing intent, neighbor notification plan, and a timeline. We help clients assemble that package before submitting.

Utility Disconnections in Woodbridge

Before demolition day, all utilities must be formally disconnected and capped at the street. For Woodbridge homes that means: Southern California Edison for electric service, SoCalGas for natural gas, and IRWD — Irvine Ranch Water District — for water and sewer. IRWD requires its own inspection and a signed release before the City will finalize the demolition permit. Lead times for IRWD scheduling can run two to three weeks; we flag this early so it does not compress your timeline.
