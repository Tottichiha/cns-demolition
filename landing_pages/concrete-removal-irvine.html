Irvine's Concrete Landscape — Why It's Actually a Straightforward Job

Irvine's master-planned design means flat lots, documented utility maps, and concrete that was poured to consistent specs across planned communities. There's no guessing where the gas main runs under a driveway the way you would in a 1940s Pasadena neighborhood. Irvine Ranch Water District's service maps, the City of Irvine's underground utility records, and HOA-maintained common area utility documentation all provide more information about what's under the concrete than you'd find in most Southern California cities.

That flat terrain also means concrete removal equipment has easy access and predictable working conditions. No hillside access issues, no narrow canyon roads, no aerial power lines at head height. The job is more physically consistent than comparable work in hillside communities — which is reflected in pricing.

What does add complexity in Irvine: shared driveways in attached-home communities, common area concrete adjacent to the work zone, and HOA specifications about how the finished surface must look after concrete is removed and replaced. The HOA approval process matters for concrete work that affects the property's exterior appearance — and in Irvine, most driveways and patios are visible from common areas or the street.

Concrete Removal Types in Irvine

Driveway Removal

Standard Irvine residential driveways — typically 4-inch unreinforced or lightly reinforced slab. Removal requires disconnection or protection of the curb cut and any HOA-maintained landscaping adjacent to the driveway edge. Full concrete hauled and disposed off-site.

Patio & Pool Deck

Poured concrete patios and pool decks throughout Irvine's older villages are frequently cracked, settled, or being replaced with pavers or new concrete. C&S removes the slab and hauls debris. Pool deck work is coordinated with the pool contractor on timing.

Garage Slab

Garage slab removal as part of garage demolition or ADU site prep. Irvine garage slabs are typically 4-inch poured with vapor barrier. Removal includes breaking, loading, and hauling. Sub-base condition confirmed after removal for your contractor.

Foundation Removal

Full foundation demolition for whole house teardown or structure removal. Irvine foundations are typically continuous perimeter with interior piers — well-documented in original construction drawings for most production-built homes. C&S removes foundation elements and leaves the site graded to your architect's spec.

Walkway & Steps

Entry walkways, side yard paths, and concrete steps throughout the property. Often removed during whole-house or major remodel projects. Access equipment navigates through side yards without damaging HOA-maintained landscaping adjacent to the property.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my HOA have to approve driveway concrete removal in Irvine?

Driveways are visible exterior features in Irvine's planned communities — which means most village ARCs require approval for removal and replacement. The HOA approval is often conditioned on the new surface matching the village's approved materials list (concrete, specific pavers, specific finishes). C&S recommends getting HOA approval for any concrete work that changes the driveway surface or footprint before scheduling demo. Interior concrete — like a garage slab — typically doesn't require ARC review.

How thick is the concrete in typical Irvine residential construction?

Irvine's production-built homes from the 1970s through 1990s typically have 4-inch residential slabs — driveways, patios, and garage floors — with occasional 6-inch thickness on driveways with heavy use. Foundations are deeper, typically 18 to 24 inches. C&S assesses actual thickness at the site walk — production specs are a useful baseline, but individual pours vary.

Is there asbestos in Irvine concrete?

Concrete itself doesn't contain asbestos. However, some vinyl floor tiles, roof coatings, and pipe insulation products applied on or adjacent to concrete surfaces may contain asbestos in pre-1979 structures. If C&S is removing a concrete floor in an older Irvine home and encounters legacy flooring materials on top of or embedded in the slab, we stop and have the material evaluated before proceeding.

Does C&S haul away all the broken concrete?

Yes. All concrete debris is loaded and hauled off-site to a licensed disposal or recycling facility. Nothing is left on the property. Concrete removal is priced to include demolition, loading, hauling, and disposal — not broken up and left in the backyard for the owner to figure out later.
