Mission Viejo Garage Demo — Two Very Different Project Types

Most garage demolition projects in Mission Viejo fall into one of two categories, and they have almost nothing in common beyond the word "garage."

Detached Garage — Usually for ADU or Backyard Project

State ADU law changed the math on backyard structures throughout South Orange County. In Mission Viejo, homeowners with detached garages that take up ADU-eligible backyard space are removing them to make room for permitted ADU construction. The demolition itself is simple — 1 to 2 days, full debris out, site graded. The permit sequencing is what needs to be planned carefully so the ADU permit can be pulled without waiting on a separate permit closure.

Mission Viejo's Building & Safety Division coordinates the demo permit with the ADU construction permit when both are submitted together. C&S advises starting this conversation with your architect and the City early — not after the ADU plans are finalized.

Attached Garage — Part of Larger Remodel or Conversion

Attached garage removal in Mission Viejo's 1970s and 1980s tract homes is more complicated than detached demo. The shared wall between the garage and the main living space, the roof tie, and the foundation interface all affect how the demolition is sequenced. The interior of the wall that's being exposed to the living space may have asbestos-containing drywall compound or texture in older homes.

C&S reviews your architect's structural drawings before any attached garage demo begins. The structural interface between the garage and the house must be clearly understood and planned for — not discovered in the middle of demo day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a garage demo require a permit in Mission Viejo?

Yes. The City of Mission Viejo requires a demolition permit for the removal of any accessory structure. The permit requires a site plan showing the structure being removed, utility disconnection documentation, and a SCAQMD Rule 1403 asbestos and lead survey — required for all structures regardless of construction date. C&S submits the permit application and manages the review process.

What happens to the concrete slab after the garage is demolished?

That depends on the next phase of the project. If an ADU is being built on the same pad and the structural engineer approves the existing slab, it may stay. If the layout is changing or the slab doesn't meet specs for the new construction, C&S removes it as part of the demolition scope. Slab disposition is confirmed before work starts — not debated at the end of the job.

My 1978 garage has a popcorn ceiling inside — does that need abatement before demo?

Popcorn ceiling texture applied before 1979 commonly contains asbestos. SCAQMD Rule 1403 requires sampling of materials that will be disturbed during demolition. If the interior ceiling texture tests positive, SCAQMD notification and abatement by a licensed contractor are required before C&S's demolition work begins. This adds time and cost — but the regulatory requirement exists regardless of the small size of the structure.

How far in advance should I schedule garage demolition in Mission Viejo?

Figure 3–4 weeks from first contact to demo completion when no abatement is required — permit processing is the main variable. If the SCAQMD survey (required for all structures regardless of age) identifies regulated materials, add time for abatement. If abatement is required, 6–8 weeks total is realistic. C&S initiates all parallel processes simultaneously to compress the timeline as much as the regulatory sequence allows.
