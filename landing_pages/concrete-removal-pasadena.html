Why Pasadena Is Different

The Root System Is the Problem, Not the Slab

Drive down any street in Bungalow Heaven, Madison Heights, or Linda Vista and count the driveways that roll, buckle, or tilt. The pattern isn't random. Pasadena planted its street trees in the early twentieth century — sycamores along Marengo and Orange Grove, coast oaks behind properties in San Rafael Hills, jacarandas on almost every block from Lake Avenue west to Rosemead. Those trees are now 80 to 100 years old, and their lateral root systems extend well past the drip line, often 30 to 50 feet from the trunk base.

When a root grows under a 4-inch concrete slab that has nowhere to flex, something gives. Sometimes it's a clean ridge — one panel lifts two or three inches. More often it's a radial crack pattern that spreads from the point of root entry across multiple panels. We've opened slabs in the Bungalow Heaven Historic District where a feeder root the diameter of a coffee can had wormed its way through 60 feet of sub-base before anyone noticed the surface crack.

This is a fundamentally different failure mode than what you see in newer construction. Marine clay expansion along the coast heaves in wet-dry cycles. Age-related deterioration in Fontana or Victorville is just concrete that's reached end of useful life. In Pasadena's established neighborhoods, the tree is still alive, still growing, and still exerting pressure. Removing the slab without understanding the root situation below it leads to new concrete that fails in the same place within five years.

Root System Note — What C&S Does and Doesn't Do

During concrete removal, C&S crews cut and remove roots that are physically in the demo zone — roots running through the slab, embedded in the sub-base, or obstructing equipment access. That's part of our scope.

Root grinding below grade, root barrier installation, arborist assessment, and chemical root treatment are tree service work. We'll tell you plainly if what we find underground warrants a call to a certified arborist before the new pour goes in. We'd rather say it than watch you repour the same slab twice.

We do a brief site walk before every concrete removal in Pasadena. We're looking at where the mature trees sit relative to the slab, whether there are visible surface root intrusions, and whether the grade near Arroyo Seco-adjacent properties is going to create drainage complications when the existing concrete comes out. None of that adds time to the estimate — it just means you're not surprised when the demo reveals something unexpected underneath.

Permits & Utilities

What the City of Pasadena Actually Requires

Most concrete removal in Pasadena — driveways, patios, pool decks, entry paths — does not require a City permit. Private flatwork on your property is generally owner-prerogative demo work, and most contractors in Pasadena will confirm this on day one. The exceptions are worth knowing.

Permit Thresholds to Know

Foundation removal falls under Pasadena's demolition permit process — even partial foundation work. Retaining walls over a certain height threshold (varies by configuration) require a grading permit from the City. Alley-adjacent work that affects the public right-of-way requires coordination with Pasadena Public Works, not a standard building permit. We'll identify which category your project falls into at the site visit — we're not going to quote you flatwork pricing on a job that's actually a grading permit situation.

Pasadena Water and Power (PWP) runs utility infrastructure through older Pasadena neighborhoods, and some of it runs beneath private driveways — particularly in pre-war residential blocks where utility routing wasn't formalized. Before any concrete removal, call 811 to have underground utilities marked. It's required by law, it's free, and it takes 48 hours. If PWP has an active line under your slab, we'll know before the breaker hits concrete.

For alley-facing properties in Northeast Pasadena and the Washington Square area: the public alley right-of-way is under City of Pasadena Public Works jurisdiction, not the property owner's. The boundary between private slab and public right-of-way isn't always obvious from the surface. We walk this line at site visit and confirm which side of it we're working on. Disruption to the public alley surface requires a separate encroachment permit — we'll tell you if your job gets there.

Common Questions

Pasadena Concrete Removal — What People Ask

The tree roots are growing through my driveway — does C&S remove the roots too?

During demo, yes — roots physically embedded in the slab or sub-base get cut and removed as part of our work. A sycamore or oak root running through the middle of your driveway doesn't stop the demo; we cut it out of the way and haul it with the concrete.

What we don't do is root grinding below the finished sub-grade, root barrier installation, or arborist work. Those are tree-service scopes, and conflating them with concrete demolition is how property owners end up paying twice for the same problem. If we open your slab and the root situation looks like it warrants an arborist consultation before the new pour goes in, we'll tell you plainly. We've been in enough Pasadena driveways to know what a root system that's going to heave again looks like.

Does concrete removal near the alley require City of Pasadena Public Works coordination?

It depends on where your private slab ends and the public right-of-way begins. In NE Pasadena and Washington Square, alley-facing garage aprons sometimes extend into the public alley — a situation that developed organically over decades and wasn't always clearly documented in original lot surveys. The public alley surface itself is under City of Pasadena Public Works jurisdiction, and any work that affects or encroaches on the alley right-of-way requires an encroachment permit, not just a standard building permit.

We walk the boundary at site visit and confirm which portion of the concrete is private and which, if any, is in the public right-of-way. If the job stays entirely on your parcel, there's no Public Works coordination needed. If the slab crosses the line, we'll tell you what the encroachment process looks like before we start. We'd rather spend twenty minutes figuring this out at the site walk than have a crew cited mid-job.

Is decorative or stamped concrete removed the same way as plain concrete?

Mechanically, yes — the removal process is the same. A stamped concrete driveway or a decorative entry path still gets saw-cut at the perimeter, broken into manageable sections, and hauled. The surface finish doesn't change the demo methodology, the equipment we use, or the scope of work.

The one practical difference is documentation. Some Pasadena property owners want a photographic or measured record of a stamped or scored pattern before it's gone — especially for historic Craftsman properties where the entry concrete was original to the house. If that's important to you, tell us at the site visit and we'll photograph the pattern and layout before the first cut. It takes fifteen minutes and costs nothing. Some clients use this for the new pour — they want to re-create the pattern in fresh concrete and need reference shots to share with their finisher.
